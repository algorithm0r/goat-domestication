# Review of exp010's scored results (2026-11-21)

On EXPLOG "exp010 results (scored)" (b926fe5), and on the tables in results/exp010_fixed-load: scored.txt,
jump_herd.txt, split_3201-4000.txt and flow_3201-4000.txt.

**Provenance.** The 14 runs started with uncommitted code were checked before scoring: 0 rows differ in every check,
and the positive control matches. Scoring followed (be1d8a2, then b926fe5).

**Predictions.** The table is what the tools print. Predictions 2, 3 and 4 are met; 1 and 5 are not. The registered
decision-rule outcome follows from the rule as written. I agree with the analysis session's argument that LOAD1 seed 5
changes no reading.

Three points concern what the paper may say from this, and two are minor.

## 1. "No divergence" at fixed loads is a herd − wild gap, and at these loads the wild moves with the herd (major for the paper's wording)
The registered measures are gaps from the wild. At the persisting fixed loads, the wild is not a fixed reference:
- **The wild's genome is mostly herd-born.** Its herd-born share is 0.886, 0.844 and 0.721 at LOADLO, LOAD05 and LOAD1,
  against REF's 0.128 (flow_3201-4000.txt, column `feral`).
- **Herd goats are lost to the wild.** 7-15% of the herd a year, against REF's 8%.
- **The herd changes; the gap hides it.** split_3201-4000.txt gives each LOAD1 seed as herd change / wild change / gap.
  R's herd change is −0.31 to −0.50 in every LOAD1 seed but seed 5. The wild follows by −0.10 to −0.19.
- **Seeds 6 and 8 show it most.** Their herds moved R by −0.308 and −0.321, nearly as far as seed 3's −0.405. Their
  gaps (−0.127 each) are small because their wild moved −0.182 and −0.194. Both sat 2600-2800 years in the small
  state, cycling goats through the herd and back out.
- **Arm means:** R's herd change is −0.374 at LOAD1 against REF's −0.714. T moves in the wild nearly as far as in the
  herd at every fixed load (LOAD1 +0.219 / +0.159), while REF's wild barely moves on either (R −0.078, T +0.012).

So the registered outcome holds as registered: no fixed load gives a persisting herd that diverges *from its wild*.
It does not mean the herd is unchanged. At fixed loads that let herds persist, the herds are open (captive-sire share
about 0.3) and lose goats to the wild, and the two pools merge.

**Fix:**
- The paper's sentence should name the reference: no divergence from the wild.
- Give herd change against the founders beside it.
- "REF's divergence needs the load to grow with the herd" should be stated as what the arms tested show. What REF has
  and the persisting fixed-load arms lack is a closed, deep herd: captive-sire share 0.064 against 0.29-0.33, depth
  14.7 against 4.0-4.7. Its herd-born wild share is also low. The scaling rule is the route to that closure in this
  model. This batch does not separate the scaling itself from the closure it gives.

## 2. The Q1 reading is about a different small state from the one Q1 asked about (moderate)
The scored reading is "the waiting does not need the load to scale with herd size". LOAD1's herds waited at 5-12 goats
(jump_herd.txt, LOAD1 rows: herd 5-12 in J−400..J−100) before jumping.

The small state behind Q1 (design review; my exp010-prereg point 2) was 20-65 goats held for thousands of years:
- RAND seeds 1 and 3: window herds 36.8 and 41.3;
- noProv seeds 1 and 2: 65.6 and 47.0 (EXPLOG, the exp005/006 per-seed lines);
- REF's own pre-jump herd: 12-66 (jump_herd.txt, REF rows), and the plateau criterion classes REF as "grew".

A 5-12-goat herd under a fixed load of 1 sits close to the capture-and-loss cycle of loads 2 and 3, which held 1-3
goats. That is the state from which LOAD1 jumps.

**Fix:** narrow the reading. "At a fixed load of 1, herds wait at 5-12 goats and then jump; a wait needs no load
scaling. Whether the 20-65-goat plateau of RAND and noProv needs scaling is not answered: no fixed-load arm sat there."

## 3. "No fixed load" covers the loads tested; the edge between 1 and 2 is unsampled (moderate)
Persistence goes from 8 of 8 at load 1 to 0 of 8 at load 2, and losses from 15% to 101% of the herd a year. No level
lies between, and the amendment's follow-up at load 4 was conditional on LOAD3 and rightly not run.

The decision rule's "no fixed load" generalizes over that unsampled interval. My expectation (a prior, not a result)
is that a persisting herd near the edge is still open, so breadth stays at 1-2. The paper should say "none of the
fixed loads tested (0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4.8)". A load of 1.5 would need its own pre-registration and should be run
only if the claim is needed in its general form.

## 4. Minor
- **Prediction 4 is prediction 2 restated.** Breadth is 1 at both exp010 levels read, and that 1 is R. The rise rests
  on R crossing 0.10 between LOADLO (exp007) and LOAD05. The entry says so; the paper must not cite 4 as separate
  support.
- **LOAD1 seed 5.** From split_3201-4000.txt's per-seed R gaps, by my unchecked hand arithmetic, the mean without
  seed 5 is about −0.27 against −0.247: still above −0.30, so no reading changes. If the number is to be recorded, it
  should come from the tool (load_tab or split_tab with a seed filter), not from this note.
- **scored.txt's median column** takes the upper of the two middle years. Label it "upper median", or have load_tab
  print jump_tab's KM median, so the table and the entry agree without a footnote.
