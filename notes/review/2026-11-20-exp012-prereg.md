# Review of the exp012 pre-registration (2026-11-20, before results)

On EXPLOG "## exp012" (5a2948f), tools/exp012.jsonl (40 runs), the cost in `src/animals.js:48-54, 235` and its one
use at `src/world.js:1649`. The arms run what the entry says: REF, CTLDEEP and CTLRAND arguments with
`reproCostFertility` 0.25 or 0.5, growth cost off. The cost is what the entry describes: per mating, conception chance
0.7 × f(dam) × f(sire), with f = 1 − c × p and p the mean of the eight core expressed traits.

As written, the decision rule can return "the grain mechanism works in goats" with no effect of the cost at all
(point 1). Points 1-3 should be fixed by amendment before any run starts.

## 1. Prediction 2 is already met at cost 0, so the decision rule cannot fail (fatal to the rule as written)
exp007's CTLDEEP, at cost 0 with the same seeds 1-8 (EXPLOG gap table, the CTLDEEP row), already meets both parts:
- **Breadth:** 6 (R A S H T W). Prediction 2 asks for at least 6.
- **Herd below wild by 0.10 or more:** R −0.165, A −0.198, H −0.195 and W −0.137, which is 4 traits. B at −0.098 just
  misses. Prediction 2 asks for at least 4.

CTLRAND at cost 0 has breadth 0, so prediction 3 is also met at cost 0. Predictions 2 and 3 together are what
CTLDEEP and CTLRAND already do. If DEEP50 and RAND50 come out the same as their cost-0 arms, the rule reports the
cost as giving the grain mechanism.

Breadth also counts the wrong thing. It is unsigned, and the cost is directional. At cost 0, CTLDEEP's S and T gaps
are positive (+0.236, +0.155): the herd drifted up toward 0.5 from a low wild mean. A cost that works should pull
those two toward 0, and if it does, breadth can fall. So prediction 2's breadth half can fail when the mechanism
succeeds.

**Fix (amendment):** state the prediction as a change from cost 0, on the quantity the cost acts on.

- **Statistic: the mean core gap.** The cost reads p, the mean of the eight core traits, so the natural statistic is
  the mean of the eight core gaps, herd p̄ − wild p̄. From the exp007 table, seed means are CTLDEEP −0.049,
  CTLRAND −0.006 and REF −0.087.
- **DEEP50 − CTLDEEP:** register it as below 0, with an unpaired 95% interval over 8 v 8 seeds that excludes 0, and
  state the size expected. (The seeds share numbers, but the trajectories part from year 0, so pairing buys nothing.)
- **RAND50 − CTLRAND:** register it as having an interval that includes 0, or as within a stated bound.
- **Per trait:** report the signed gap change. Predict that S and T fall from their cost-0 values.
- **Decay in the grain sense:** this is herd change against the founders that is negative and below cost 0's. Read it
  through the within-arm split, which the measure already lists, as well as through herd − wild.
- **Breadth:** keep it as a secondary, not in the decision rule.

## 2. The cost halves conception as well as selecting; add a flat-cost arm (major)
On 2026-11-03 the steward computed that this cost at 0.5 takes the mean conception chance per mating from 0.7 to about
0.7 × (1 − 0.5 × 0.56)² ≈ 0.36 (EXPLOG, the "No genome cost" entry). DEEP50 therefore differs from CTLDEEP in two
ways:
- the gradient on p;
- a near-halving of conceptions, which moves herd growth, generation time, captures, culls and time to establishment.

Either could move the gaps, and the design can't tell them apart. exp011 met the same problem with FLAT.

**Fix:** add DEEPFLAT50, CTLDEEP's arguments with `reproCostFertility` 0 and `matingConception` lowered to give the
same mean conception chance with no gradient. Register its value now, 0.7 × (1 − 0.5 × p̄)², with p̄ taken from
CTLDEEP's cost-0 population; that is about 0.36 if p̄ ≈ 0.56. Then read the cost's selective effect as
DEEP50 − DEEPFLAT50. That is 8 runs. RANDFLAT50 would complete the contrast and is worth adding if the queue allows.

## 3. The design does not show the cost selects; add the selection differential on p (major)
The cost acts per heat, and a doe that fails cycles again. She is in heat 1 day in 21 within a season of half-width
15 + 120 × T days (`animals.js:243-246`, params.js:92-95).

My arithmetic below is unchecked against model output. At p 0.5 versus 0.4, the chance per heat is 0.39 versus 0.45.
That is about 2.5 versus 2.2 heats to conceive, or a delay of roughly 7 days. Most does in a season of several cycles
still conceive. If so, lifetime kids are lost mainly through that delay and through does that miss the whole
season, and the gradient on fitness is much weaker than the gradient per mating. The only prior evidence is weak in
the same direction: on 2026-11-03, turning off both costs at 0.5 (fertility and growth) moved the wild's summed load
only from 4.45 to 4.51 (H0) and from 4.53 to 4.74 (H1). The model has changed a great deal since then.

If the cost barely selects, a null on point 1 means "the cost does not select here". It does not mean "closure plus
a cost does not give decay", and the decision rule would read it as the second.

**Fix:** register the herd's selection differential on p. This is the mean over the eight core traits of `selDam` and
of `selSire` (`world.js:1486-1501`), kids-weighted over 3201-4000. Compare DEEP50 with a cost-0 baseline that carries
the counters. exp009's CTLDEEP has them: 16 seeds, code 6367599, cost 0, with C shifted, and C is read by nothing.
State that a DEEP50 differential within noise of the baseline makes any null on point 1 "the cost does not select",
not a test of the mechanism.

**Question:** does any counter give conceptions per heat, or does-seasons with no conception, by herd and wild? If one
does, it is the direct check on the arithmetic above.

## 4. Under a cost per heat, T buys conceptions back; predict its sign separately (moderate)
T widens the breeding season (`animals.js:243`), so it gives more heats per season. A lower chance per heat raises
the value of extra heats. The cost therefore selects T upward through season length and downward through p, and the
net sign is not given.

On 2026-11-03 the wild's T was higher with the costs on than off: 0.37 against 0.25 (H0) and 0.42 against 0.28 (H1).
In that comparison only 3 of 8 wild traits were lower with the cost by 0.05 or more:
- H0: A 0.77 against 0.85, M 0.17 against 0.23, W 0.58 against 0.72;
- H1: A, M and W again.

Prediction 1 (at least 5 of 8 lower by 0.05) is therefore at risk, and a failure on T would be the model working, not
the cost failing.

**Fix:** predict T's sign on its own. Leave T out of the count in prediction 1 and out of any "decay" count, or state
its expected direction with this reason.

## 5. The cost-0 controls ran on older code; one long identity run would settle it (moderate)
The cost-0 baselines are REF from exp005 and exp006, and CTLDEEP and CTLRAND from exp007 at a0a2e12. exp012 runs at
a659a59. Each change between them is identity-checked over 500 years (EXPLOG b64a201, 1fbba56). With herdingAt 400,
500 years is only 100 years of herding. aab0e86's switches act only on herds, and they had a tenth of the herd time
to show a difference.

**Fix:** ask the steward for one 4000-year run of CTLDEEP seed 1 at cost 0 on a659a59, compared year by year with
exp007's. This check can fail; the 500-year checks could barely fail on herd code. If it differs, the cost-0 arms must
be re-run on a659a59 (16 runs). That would also give same-code counters for point 3.

## 6. The decision rule leaves outcomes undefined (moderate)
- **"No more directional than at cost 0" has no number.** Use point 1's statistic and interval.
- **DEEP50 directional, but RAND50 directional too.** This outcome is not covered. The mean core gap in RAND50 falling
  would mean the cost acts on the herd beyond what flows in from the wild, which is not closure's doing.
- **Prediction 1 fails, and the wild hardly moves.** This outcome is not covered. Read it with point 3's differential
  before reading points 1 and 2.
- **The fallback to 0.25.** The rule says "the reading uses 0.25" if herds fail at 0.5, but no prediction is stated
  at 0.25. State 2 and 3 at 0.25 too, or say that 0.25 is read only for its sign.

## 7. REF25 and REF50 have no prediction (minor)
This is the same gap as exp009 point 4.

**Fix:** state one. At cost 0, REF's culture holds R and B against the wild (gaps −0.636 and −0.295 in exp007).
Its other core gaps are smaller (A −0.148, H −0.083, M −0.062, W −0.090) and are the ones open to the cost. A
prediction could be:
- R and B gaps within 0.05 of cost 0;
- the mean of the other six gaps falls by a stated amount.
