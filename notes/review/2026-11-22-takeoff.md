tools/takeoff_tab.py at 7e44e2a (header read). I ran `selftest`, which passes, and `selftest bad`, which fails on the

The result follows the reading stated before the data was read:
- LOAD1's 7 waiting seeds read **mixed**: R first in 3, size first in 2, no first in 2.
- The exp005 runs are reported apart, as the clarification says.

The analysis session's four caveats are all true. The entry also says that the order is unresolved in 5 of the 7 seeds

What follows limits what even "mixed" can carry.

## 1. A departure test across variables with different noise partly measures the noise (major, for any reading of order)
Each variable departs when it moves beyond 2 SD of its own plateau. The plateaus are not alike:
- **R** is the herd's genotype mean (`datamanager.js:93`, genotypic values) over 5-12 goats, and each capture or death
  moves it.
- **Size** is a count.
- **Does** are size times a share, so they move with size by construction. That is the analysis session's point (1).

A noisier plateau crosses its 2 SD bar later for the same underlying change. So "first" mixes the order of change with
each variable's signal-to-noise. The tilt has two parts:
- R's noise pushes R's departure late.
- The alignment, with J set on size, pushes size's departure early.

Both tilt the test against "R first". A tie or an R lag of under about 50 years (s1 +42, s4 +54) therefore can't tell
the two hypotheses apart.

**Fix (exploratory, beside the stated reading):**
- Print each variable's plateau SD relative to its J..J+300 change. This shows how far each 2 SD bar is from that
  variable's own move.
- Add a reading on a matched scale: for each variable, the first year it has covered a fixed fraction (say a quarter)
  of its own plateau-to-post change and stays there. The fraction is to be stated before reading.
- Neither changes the stated "mixed".

## 2. Herd R falls with growth by composition alone (moderate)
Herd R averages captives, carrying the wild's R, with home-born goats, carrying the herd's. As home-born kids
accumulate, the herd mean moves toward the home-born value whether or not anything selects. So R falling with size, or
just after it, is what the demographic hypothesis predicts. Only R falling well before size and does is specific to
feedback.

The table shows R leading by no more than 15 years, within the smoothing window. The supportable sentence is: "in no
LOAD1 seed does the herd's R fall ahead of its growth by more than the resolution". Point 1 weakens even that.

## 3. The feedback loop's own link is loss, and loss can't be read here (moderate)
The feedback hypothesis says a less reactive herd loses fewer goats and so grows. Its direct signature is loss falling
before size rises. The registered reading uses R instead, and loss departs before J only in s4 (−75). In three seeds it
departs after J, and in three it never departs.

The loss plateau is escaped per herd goat in a herd of 5-12, so the series is mostly zeros and ones. An absent
departure there is a limit of the instrument, not evidence that loss didn't fall. The plot's fall "from about J−150" is
read by eye, and the entry should not state it as a result.

**Fix:**
- Say that the loop's mechanism, loss, is not resolved at these herd sizes.
- If the question matters to the paper, the instrument is a longer pooled window or loss counts summed over seeds. It
  is not this departure test.

## 4. Minor

  The question "what ends the plateau" can't be asked of them. The 12 runs that pass the 20-99 rule grow through that

## What this licenses for Chris's question
"What triggers the phase transition?" isn't answered by this batch's records. At LOAD1's herd sizes, R, size and
breeding does move together within 15 years. That is inside the 30-year smoothing window, and the test favours size.
Nothing here shows R falling first. The data can't exclude a feedback loop either: its link, loss, is too sparse in
herds of 5-12 to time.

3. **A finer test in existing records.** Less smoothing won't resolve order: the limit is R's noise in 5-12 goats, not

   - **Run-offs** (`ranOff`, present in every LOAD1 year row; I checked the key, not the values): run-offs per herd

   - **Pooled, matched-scale timing:** each variable's first crossing of a fixed fraction of its own change on the
     seed-pooled aligned trajectory, with the fraction stated before reading.
   Both are exploratory and should be stated before the tool reads the data, as 72bcc06 was.

## On the matched-scale results (4650a89, takeoff_tab 8ae956e)
The scale limit the analysis session raised after reading is a flaw in the instrument I proposed in a5a0b06: a fixed
fraction of a variable's change on a linear scale reaches an exponentially growing herd (5-12 to about 246) late by
construction, so R's quarter point precedes size's under either trigger. I did not see it when I proposed it. "R first
in 7 of 7" on this scale is therefore not evidence for feedback, and the entry says so; noting it after reading without
changing the measure is the right handling. The noise ratios (R 0.03-0.07, size and does 0.00-0.02) bear out my point
1, though the tilt is smaller than I implied: R's 2-SD bar sits at 6-14% of its own change. Loss's quarter points
(three seeds before R) carry noise ratios of 0.32-0.78 and should not be read. The stated reading stays mixed, and the
steward's close of this test stands; a log-size version would be a new measure, not a repair, and is not worth adding.
