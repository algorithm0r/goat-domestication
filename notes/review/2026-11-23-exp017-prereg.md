## 1. Prediction 1 and its decision clause confuse density with the filter's own success (major, your (d))
The catch chance is the receiver's alarmSpread × R × fearOfHumans (world.js, spreadAlarm: `rand() >= P.alarmSpread *
n.tr[T_R] * n.fearOfHumans`). So the cascade from one spook shrinks as the herd's R × fear falls, which is the filter
working.

Prediction 1 bins year rows over 401-4000 by herd size. The 200+ bin is mostly late history, after selection. The 1-9
bin is mostly early, at founder R. A level whose filter works well can therefore show smaller cascades in big herds
than density alone would give, and could fail prediction 1. The decision rule then says "that level's reading is not
about density", which would be the wrong inference in exactly the case where spreading worked.

**Fix (amendment):**
- Read prediction 1 over 401-1200, as the probe did, when R has moved least.
- Print 401-4000 beside it as descriptive.
- Say how seeds combine: counts summed over all of a level's rows across seeds, as spread_probe sums within a file.
  "Rows" are recorded year rows.
- Say that prediction 1 was seen to hold in the probe (seed 1 at 0.1, 0.3 and 1). It is a mechanism check, not a test.

The levels were chosen on cascade size and herd size, not on this line, and that choice is legitimate calibration.

## 4. Your (a): the cascade is a fair contagion model; three things to state (minor)
- **The receiver's R × fear** matches the herder spook (animals.js:369), so the two filters select on the same thing.
  The sender's state rightly doesn't matter: a bolting goat is a bolting goat.
- **Dose counts.** Each bolter re-queues its cell, and every goat in the 3×3 that hasn't bolted gets a fresh draw. A
  goat with more bolting neighbours is more likely to catch the alarm, which is the right form for contagion.
- **The 3×3 radius is a free choice,** and it sets where the density effect saturates. The probe already shows the
  saturation: 2.03 against 2.09 (0.1) and 3.30 against 3.11 (0.3) at 100-199 and 200+. The herder-load factor grows in
  proportion to the herd (world.js:226). So at the herd sizes that persist, the spreading acts as a roughly constant
  multiplier on 0.5, a step somewhere between 30 and 100 goats rather than a proportional rise. Say so beside branch 1,
  and scope the result to radius 1. My guess is that the saturation comes from the herd filling its herdRange 8; I
  have not checked that.
- **Positions from the index (small, no change).**
  - A goat is found by its cell at the sub-tick's start, but it bolts and passes the alarm on from where it is now
    (`nx = n.x`). A goat that stepped earlier in the sub-tick has moved up to one cell by day.
  - Goats earlier in the step order get a graze move and a bolt in the same sub-tick; later ones get only the bolt
    (animals.js:271).
  - Neither effect is more than one cell or tied to R.
- **Caught goats are set alarmed and learn fear (alarmLearn),** so kids learn from caught mothers (animals.js:277-278)
  as they do from spooked ones. That is consistent with the spook.

## 5. Your (b): the exclusions are the spook's own set; no bias against LOAD05 (minor)
walksWithHerder mirrors step()'s route to stepWithHerd (animals.js:271-287): led, sheltered or kept at camp, a kid with
its mother, loose. Those are exactly the goats the herder can't spook. So the catch set equals the spook set, and
kids escape both filters at LOAD05 as at every SPR level. That is inherited, not new.

The only difference: a loose goat back within keepRadius rejoins in its own step(), so until then walksWithHerder reads
it as loose. That is one sub-tick and negligible.
