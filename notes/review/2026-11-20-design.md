## Fatal

### 1. The R gap is a readout of herd size that the spook rule writes in; every R-based claim restates that rule
**Element.** The herd's run-off hazard is proportional to R and to herd size:
- `src/animals.js:363`: `spooked = ... rand() < (P.herderAlarm / spookSteps) * R * this.fearOfHumans * world.herdLoad(this.herd)`
- `src/world.js:181-183`: `herdLoad(camp) { ... return Math.max(PARAMETERS.minLoad, this.herdCount[camp] / (PARAMETERS.herdPerHerder * ...)) }` (herdPerHerder 20, minLoad 0.25; `params.js:249-250`)
- a run-off is lost by the approach test, again in R: `world.js:758`: `p = Math.min(1, PARAMETERS.stalkDetect * g.tr[T_R] * g.fearOfHumans)`.

So the strength of selection on R per goat rises linearly with the number of goats in the camp's herd. My arithmetic:
REF's 288 goats over 3 camps give a load of about 4.8 per herder; a 30-goat herd (10 a camp) gives 0.5. The per-goat
spook hazard differs about tenfold between the two states.

**How it fails.** The R gap has one value in every herd that grows: "In every seed of every arm other than RAND s1-s4
and noProv s1-s2, R is -0.55 to -0.68 in every window" (EXPLOG 5207). It is -0.57 to -0.65 across linear and curved
growth, every growth credit and every feedCost (EXPLOG 4823-4826, 4873-4877, 4907, 5068-5072), and -0.03 to -0.24 only
where herds stayed small (exp002 TS herds 20, -0.03, EXPLOG 5017; exp001 KAH, -0.24 with two seeds at 29 and 35 goats,
EXPLOG 4960-4961). The R gap therefore carries one bit (did the herd grow), and its size is set by herderAlarm,
herdPerHerder, stalkDetect and the habituated fear, none of which batch 1 sweeps. Claims that rest on it:
- "est" is defined as R gap ≤ -0.30 (`tools/corner_tab.py:5,44`; EXPLOG 5237). Establishment and the R result are the
  same variable.
- r(herd, R gap) = -0.91 to -0.99 (outline 266) and -0.94 (outline 287) are the herdLoad line, not findings.
- The goat-grain comparison (outline 358; draft Discussion): "goats pooled r = -0.87, lineage arms -0.89" are
  correlations of depth with the **R** gap, set against the grain r = 0.79. The outline itself says "R is filtered in
  every persistent herd, so it is not the measure isolation is tested on" (outline 496). The Discussion row contradicts
  that sentence.
- 3.3, "Small herds with no gap, large herds with a gap near -0.6" (outline 264), presents as a finding the two states
  of a rule whose filter strength is proportional to herd size.

**Severity.** Fatal for 3.3, for every "established in k of 8" used as evidence, and for the Table 3 depth-gap row.

**Fix / evidence that would settle it.** (a) Define establishment by herd size and persistence (for example herd ≥ 100
for 400 consecutive years), never by a trait gap. (b) Ask the steward for one check arm, REF with `herdPerHerder=1e9`
(load fixed at minLoad, so the spook hazard no longer scales with herd size). If the R gap of established herds falls
well below -0.6 and r(herd, R gap) weakens, the R result is the load rule and the paper should say so in Methods. If it
holds, this finding drops to major. (c) Sweep herderAlarm (the 2026-10 sweep_spook numbers, MODEL.md 542-547, show gaps
of -0.04 to -0.24 across rates on the old model) and report R as conditional on it. (d) Drop R from every isolation
correlation, as the outline already says for the lineage block.

### 2. The lineage test cannot separate isolation from selection already done in the herd, or from the wild moving away
**Element.** Outline 296: "under one herding regime, the lineage alone sets the breadth of the syndrome. This is the
causal core." Draft abstract, line 36 (and lines 109-110, 724): "breeding the herd from its deepest lineage, with no
trait under selection, moves most of the traits we measure". The deep rule picks sires by `-g.wildDepth()`
(`world.js:918`).

**How it fails.** Two routes produce directional gaps under the deep rule, and neither is isolation as such.
1. *Accumulated within-herd selection.* Isolation cannot by itself move a mean in a consistent direction across seeds;
   drift has mean zero. A consistent sign needs selection. The herd regime selects on R (finding 1), on S (the herd
   pull `(0.5 + S)` in `stepWithHerd`, `animals.js:384`, should keep high-S goats near the herder and so out of the stray
   test) and on B, T and L through the keep value (`world.js:1188-1240`). The sires with the most herd generations
   carry the most rounds of that selection. Deep against random is then "sires from the most-selected lines against
   average sires". "No trait under selection" is true of the sire rule and false of the regime, and the abstract says it
   of the experiment.
2. *The wild moves; an isolated herd does not follow.* The gap is herd minus a wild that keeps evolving. Outline 204-206:
   under wolves alone, from 201-300 to 1501-1600, "S moves -0.27, W +0.17, L +0.16, H +0.12, M +0.11 and A +0.08". The
   deep arm's gaps (outline 301-303) are A -0.11, S +0.18, H -0.21, W -0.10: each the opposite sign of the wild's own
   movement, which is what a herd that stood still while the wild moved would show. (Both sets are old-model numbers;
   the point is the test, not the values.)

**Severity.** Fatal for contribution 1 as worded and for the abstract's sentence. The result may survive as "isolation
lets within-herd selection accumulate and lets the herd stop tracking the wild", which is migration-selection balance
and needs to be argued as that, with the textbook expectation stated.

**Fix / evidence.** (a) Decompose every gap into herd change and wild change, each against the founders' mean at year
400 (the histograms exist; this needs an analysis tool, not runs). If the deep arm's A, S, H, W gaps are mostly wild
change, the "syndrome" is the wild's. (b) A control arm in which the herd regime selects on no trait: deep, random and
shallow sires with `herderAlarm=0` and the random cull (so no spook filter and no keep value). Prediction if gaps are
selection carried by isolation: seed-mean gaps near zero, larger per-seed spread in the deep arm. Ask the steward to add
it to B4 before launch. (c) Remove "with no trait under selection" from draft.tex 36, 109-110 and 724,
or qualify it to "with no trait chosen by the sire rule".

### 3. Trait co-change across sweep cells is manufactured by the herd/no-herd mixture
**Element.** Outline 52-54 (contribution 3): the sweep "shows how far the herd's isolation ties the trait changes
together". Outline 284-285 (Fig. 5B): "the trait-gap correlation matrix across cells, raw and with depth and herd size
partialled out. The syndrome without pleiotropy shows up as co-change that falls when depth is held fixed."

**How it fails.** Cells whose herds never grow have every gap at zero: NONE and every add-one arm but addCull have gaps
within 0.04 of zero on every trait (EXPLOG 5275-5284). Cells whose herds grow have many gaps away from zero. Across such
a set every pair of trait gaps correlates, because both are zero in one cluster and nonzero in the other. The
preliminary pooled table shows this structure: 115 of 416 runs at depth 0-2 with herd 27 and every gap 0.00 to -0.06
(outline 554). Partialling herd size or depth out of a bimodal mixture does not remove a cluster effect; it fits a line
through two clouds. The 2^8 factorial will contain many cells that never establish: from the bare culture only the cull
started herds, in 4 of 8 seeds (EXPLOG 5276).

**Severity.** Fatal for Fig. 5B and for the "isolation ties the traits together" half of contribution 3.

**Fix.** Estimate co-change within established herds only (by finding 1's herd-size definition), across cells and across
seeds within cells; report how many cells and seeds that leaves. Report the establishment outcome separately (finding 7).
Give the null: the correlation matrix expected if each trait moved independently given establishment.

## Major

### 4. The B gap is a modeller's choice of herder belief, chosen after the outcome was seen, and it runs to the clamp
**Element.** `world.js:1177` sets the reference size, `world.js:1191` credits growth toward it
(`g.curveGainTo(ref[g.sex], GD)`), and the sires are the oldest surviving bucks (`world.js:1167, 1492`:
`.sort((a, b) => b.age - a.age).slice(0, P.keepSires)`). A large-B buck reaches the herd mean sooner, is credited no
growth, falls below the threshold and is eaten; a small-B buck is credited growth, survives, ages and becomes a sire.

**How it fails.** (a) B's gap is set by which reference the modellers give the herder: own target +0.03, size at
slaughter -0.16, herd mean from 2 y -0.21, from 3 y -0.26, from 4 y -0.57, largest goat -0.67, linear herd credit -0.82
(EXPLOG 4823-4826, 4873-4877, 4907). (b) The default ('herd') was adopted on 2026-11-18 (EXPLOG 4839-4842) after the
curved growth rule had removed the B gap (EXPLOG 4608-4609) and after the crossover was run with the stated hope that
"our new one should gain it" (EXPLOG 4794). The record is open about this; the paper must be too. (c) B does not settle:
REF's worst trait at 3200 is B (EXPLOG 5322), the bias SEs over seeds are 0.04-0.21 (EXPLOG 5187), and single seeds reach
-0.74 to -0.78 (REF s2, noTether s2, noGuard s4; EXPLOG 5195-5202). With wild B about 0.87 (EXPLOG 5111) that is a herd B
near 0.1, my arithmetic, close to the allele clamp at 0 (`genome.js:11`). The old headline B gap was at the clamp: LH
herd B 0.055 (EXPLOG 4823). A trait run down to the clamp by a reference that tracks its own herd mean measures run
length and the clamp, not a stable outcome. (d) B counts in "breadth" and in the co-change analysis.

**Fix.** Present body size only as the B5 sensitivity result: "if herders judge growth against the herd's own mean, size
falls without limit; against the goat's own target, it does not". Report breadth with and without B. Give the B
trajectory, not a window mean. Report the herder-reference choice and its date in Methods.

### 5. The two-corner design cannot show interactions, and the leave-one-out map is confounded with herd size
**Element.** EXPLOG 5239: "'interacts' = core traits where add and drop differ by more than 0.10". Outline 24-26 (banner):
"the selection map comes from leave-one-out".

**How it fails.** (a) Add effects are measured from a culture whose herds never grow (NONE herd 11.4, est 0;
EXPLOG 5275), so every add effect is about zero and every drop effect over 0.10 is listed as an interaction by
definition. The "interacts" column (EXPLOG 5300-5317) is a list of drop effects, not interactions. (b) The drop effects
change herd size by -210 to +178 goats (noPen +210 → 497; noProv -178; EXPLOG 5303, 5307). Since R's filter (finding 1),
drift (Ne) and the keep value's camp records all scale with herd size, a drop effect mixes the behaviour's own selective
action with its demographic effect. Example: dropping penning shifts B H T M L (EXPLOG 5303) and doubles the herd; the
design cannot say which. (c) The 2^8 factorial (batch1-plan 33; outline banner 33) inherits both problems: main effects
average over cells with and without herds.

**Fix.** (a) Drop the "interacts" label for the two-corner data. (b) Run the key drop arms with herd size held at
REF's by the existing cap (`herdTarget`, `params.js:225`, which culls the lowest keep values above the cap), or at a
common cap for all arms; then a drop effect at fixed herd size is the behaviour's selective action. (c) For the
factorial, model two outcomes: time to establishment (finding 7), and gaps conditional on establishment with herd size as
a covariate, stated as a mediator.

### 6. "Cull off" is a 13% random quota, matched to the threshold cull only at REF
**Element.** batch1-plan 11: the random rule at "the anchor's own rate (37.8 culled a year from a herd of 286, about
13%), so the factor tests *which* goats die, not *how many*". EXPLOG 5226: cull off = "random cull at quota 0.13".

**How it fails.** The rates match only in REF. The threshold cull eats only goats whose keep value is below zero, so its
rate adapts to the herd; the quota takes 13% whatever the herd's state. In the bare culture a 13% random offtake on
herds of about 11 goats is a fixed sink; the threshold cull removes it. addCull's herd of 130 against NONE's 11
(EXPLOG 5275-5276) may be "no fixed quota" rather than "which goats die". The banner's "from the bare culture only the
threshold cull starts a herd" (outline 25) cannot distinguish the two.

**Fix.** Report realized offtake per year (culled / herd) for every arm. Add a bottom-corner control with no offtake at
all (or the quota at the rate addCull realizes). Ask the steward whether either is cheap enough to add to B1.

### 7. Establishment is a censored waiting time, counted at an arbitrary end, from 8 seeds
**Element.** "Established in k of 8" (outline 28-29); est by arm (EXPLOG 5275-5294).

**How it fails.** RAND is 1 of 8 at 1600 and 4 of 8 at 4000 (EXPLOG 5319-5321, 5287); addGuard's one established seed
jumped after year 2400 (EXPLOG 5342). The count is a property of where the run stops. With 8 seeds the binomial 95%
intervals are wide (my arithmetic, Clopper-Pearson): 4 of 8 is 0.16-0.84; 0 of 8 is 0-0.37. "addPen 0 of 8" and
"addCull 4 of 8" are not distinguishable at that resolution by count alone.

**Fix.** Treat establishment as time to event: record each seed's jump year (first year of the herd-size criterion),
report Kaplan-Meier curves or a hazard per arm with intervals, and state that seeds unjumped at 4000 are censored.
The analysis session's `tools/jump_tab.py` (uncommitted when I read it) builds the Kaplan-Meier part; its jump criterion
is an R-gap threshold (`--thr -0.30`), which finding 1 argues against. Use herd size. If the
paper wants a probability of establishment, it needs more seeds per arm than the gap estimates do; that is cheaper if a
run can stop once it has jumped and its post-jump window is collected (a question for the steward).

### 8. The settling rule cannot certify 4000 years, and its tolerance is below its noise
**Element.** EXPLOG 5141-5142: "the suite's run length = the latest settled-at over the counted arms; if any counted arm
is not settled at 3200, 4000".

**How it fails.** (a) The rule cannot return a length above 4000, so "4000" means "not shown to settle", not "settled".
REF itself is not settled (EXPLOG 5208-5209, 5322). (b) The criterion is |bias| ≤ 0.05 while B's bias SEs are 0.04-0.21
(EXPLOG 5187): a pass or fail at that SE is close to chance. (c) For bistable arms a window mean is a mixture of seeds
before and after the jump; it "settles" only when no seed has jumps left, which is a property of the hazard, not of
equilibrium within herds.

**Fix.** Say in Methods that the runs are not at equilibrium for B and the minor traits, give trajectories, and make
claims about direction and order, not end values. Separate within-state settling (post-jump seeds only) from mixture
settling.

### 9. Eight seeds resolve only large effects on B, and nothing controls for the number of comparisons
**Element.** Eight seeds per arm (batch1-plan 33-38); the effects table (EXPLOG 5298-5317).

**How it fails.** REF's B gap at 3801-4000 by seed is -0.11, -0.74, -0.27, -0.12 (EXPLOG 5195): SD about 0.30, my
arithmetic. To get a 95% half-width of 0.05 on a difference between two arms with that spread needs about 2 ×
(1.96 × 0.30 / 0.05)² ≈ 280 seeds per arm, my arithmetic. At 8 seeds the B drop effects have SEs 0.07-0.12 (EXPLOG
5301-5317). The table holds 9 behaviours × 2 corners × 9 traits = 162 estimates; at the 5% level about 8 would pass by
chance. exp006 prediction 5 "not met" (addExchREF B 0.159 from REF, EXPLOG 5368) is within two SEs of the exchange drop
effect's own SE (0.10, EXPLOG 5317). The lineage block's CI rule (outline 497-499: half-width 0.03 on A, S, B, H, W) was
sized on the old Ldeep SD 0.065; if B's spread under the deep and random sire rules is anything like REF's 0.30, the
rule asks for several hundred seeds (my arithmetic) and will not stop.

**Fix.** Pre-register a seed cap for the CI rule and the trait set it applies to. State which effects the paper will
interpret (a short list, fixed before the data) and treat the rest as exploratory with a multiplicity correction or a
false-discovery statement. Take the per-seed SD of each gap in REF from exp005/exp006, and from a short pilot for the other sire rules, before
fixing seeds.

### 10. The gap is measured against a hunted, moving, feral-mixed wild
**Element.** Gap = herd − wild, with wild = all goats minus herd goats (`tools/settle_tab.py:4-5`: "wild = hist -
histHerd").

**How it fails.** (a) Wild R rises under hunting from about 0.62 to about 0.86 (MODEL.md 526; outline 200), so some of
REF's R gap of -0.636 is the wild's rise, not the herd's fall. (b) The wild drifts on other traits (finding 2). (c) "Wild"
includes escaped herd goats and their descendants; the model tracks this (`wildHerdShare`, MODEL.md 482-483) but the gap
does not use it. A referee in domestication studies will ask "diverged from what?": the founders, the pre-human wild,
or the present wild near camps?

**Fix.** Report three numbers per trait: herd minus founders (wild at year 400), wild now minus founders, and herd minus
wild-without-herd-ancestry. A tool over the existing histograms (`histDepth` splits wild goats by herd-born share,
MODEL.md 480-482).

### 11. Isolation, the paper's subject, is not measured directly
**Element.** Title and frame (outline 41-43, draft abstract 39-40): "an isolation of the managed lineage that survives
continuing gene flow". Measures listed at outline 181-189: gaps, depth, IBD, captures, conceptions by origin.

**How it fails.** (a) Depth is genome share by herd-born generations (MODEL.md 474-479); under the deep sire rule the
treatment selects on the measure. (b) The neutral marker's **mean** gap is near zero by expectation under any degree of
isolation (drift has no direction): REF C -0.001 (EXPLOG 5285). Its per-seed spread, about ±0.1 (batch1-plan 53-54), is
the signal of isolation, and nobody plans to report it as such. (c) "Wild bucks sire about 1% of herd births" (outline
57) is an old-model number. (d) On the old model about 61% of captures were recaptured herd escapees (MODEL.md 550-551),
so counting captures as wild gene flow overstates it. Whether that holds on v0.1.0 is unknown.

**Fix.** Report F_ST (or the per-seed |C gap| and the C distribution's divergence) for the neutral marker, and gene flow
as migrants per generation by ancestry (wv / herd-born share of each captured goat and of each wild sire), not by capture
event. Re-measure the wild-sired share on v0.1.0 from the conceptions-by-origin records.

### 12. The kill-off calibration fails the targets the paper will cite, and the outline cites stale passes
**Element.** Outline 174: "Ganj Dareh: does past 2.5 y 0.66-0.69 ✓; males dead before 2.5 y 0.58". Section 3.3b
(outline 271-277).

**How it fails.** On v0.1.0 the anchor meets 1 of 4 Ganj Dareh targets on slaughter and 0 of 4 on all causes: does dead
at 2.5 y or older 0.45 against 0.60-0.70; F:M 0.93 (all causes) and 0.15 (slaughter) against 1.11-1.86; aged 4+ 0.11-0.14
against about 0.20 (EXPLOG 5113-5114). The one target met, males dead before 2.5 y, was tuned to it ("once chosen, the
male kill age is a calibration target, not a test", EXPLOG 5045). The out-of-sample checks at the calibration point were
0 of 3 (EXPLOG 5077-5079). A Zeder-school referee will read the herd as killing males almost exclusively.

**Fix.** Update 2.8 to v0.1.0 numbers, report the misses in the main text, and drop 3.3b's "which cultures reproduce
the Ganj Dareh profile" unless the sweep finds cultures that meet more than the tuned target.

### 13. MODEL.md, which the paper's ODD and Table 1 come from, describes a different model in places
**Element.** MODEL.md 126-128 (C22): "Growth rate × (1 − 0.5p) ... Each mating's conception chance × (1 − 0.5 p_doe) ×
(1 − 0.5 p_buck). Unconditional". `params.js:45-47`: `reproCostGrowth: 0 ... off by default since 2026-11-03`,
`reproCostFertility: 0`. Also MODEL.md 411 ("Offtake 10%/y in H2–H6"; batch 1 has no quota), 485-491 (§9 calibration
dated 2026-10-21), C23/C24 in forage units where params are in kcal, and the code comment `animals.js:16` and 47-49
("a trait whose benefit is removed decays instead of drifting") describing a cost that is off.

**How it fails.** A referee who reads the ODD and then the code finds the genome cost, the cull and the calibration
described wrongly. Outline 111-113 builds Table 1 and Algorithm 1 from MODEL §2-3 and S1 is "the ODD".

**Fix.** The steward regenerates MODEL.md for v0.1.0 (costs off, threshold cull, curved growth, herd credit, defaults
with values) before the author writes Methods from it.

### 14. No inbreeding depression, in herds whose IBD is 0.90
**Element.** Outline 383 lists it as a limitation. exp004: herd IBD 0.90 (EXPLOG 5111); old deep arm 0.91 (outline 302).

**How it fails.** The deep and long-isolated herds the paper credits with the syndrome are exactly the herds that real
inbreeding depression would shrink or end, and Daly et al. 2021, which the introduction cites for the narrow Y line,
also report the inbreeding. Without a cost the model has no counter-pressure to isolation, so "isolation survives" is
partly assumed.

**Fix.** A sensitivity arm with a fertility or kid-survival cost of IBD in the lineage block, or a Discussion paragraph
that states the direction of the bias and why the conclusion survives it.

### 15. The base settings were chosen to maximise the effect
**Element.** Outline 123-124: "range 8 maximises herd-wild separation so the evidence is clearest, and the range
dependence is reported honestly." On the old base, breadth was 3 / 3 / 8 at ranges 4 / 6 / 8 (outline 482-484).

**How it fails.** Choosing the base condition by the size of the outcome is a researcher degree of freedom a referee
will name. Breadth more than doubled between range 6 and 8.

**Fix.** B7 must report the paper's main claims (not only herd size and gaps) at ranges 4 and 6, and the Methods must
say the base range was chosen for separation. If a claim holds only at range 8, it is a range-8 claim.

### 16. The mutation step, heritability 1 and no expression noise set every rate, and nothing varies them
**Element.** `params.js:383`: `mutationStep: 0.04, // uniform ±step on every allele at meiosis`; MODEL.md C20 "No
expression noise"; outline 384 "Heritability 1".

**How it fails.** Every allele moves at every meiosis. The speed of every response, the B ratchet's pace, the neutral
marker's drift yardstick and the clamp effects all scale with this step, which comes from the grain model ("the grain
model's value"), not from goats. A quantitative genetics referee will ask for the mutational variance per generation and
how it compares with estimates for mammals.

**Fix.** One sensitivity arm at half and double the step on REF and the lineage arms, or present results as signs and
orders only and say why.

## Minor

### 17. Breadth is a thresholded count
Outline 183: "breadth = traits with |gap| ≥ 0.10". The outline already notes traits at -0.095 and -0.098 (outline
325-326). Report the gap vector with intervals and a continuous summary (sum of |gap|, or a distance scaled by the
neutral marker's spread) beside the count.

### 18. The pleiotropy arm's answer is the arithmetic of the mixing rule
`genome.js:109-110`: `out[T_W] = (1 - P.pleioW) * out[T_W] + P.pleioW * out[T_R]` (and A likewise). With the R gap near
-0.6 in established herds, the W gap under pleioW = p is about (1 − p) × W gap + p × (−0.6) before any selection response.
Pre-register that arithmetic as the prediction, so the paper reports the deviation from it, not the obvious part.

### 19. Gaps are computed from 20-bin histograms, and only over years with a herd
`settle_tab.py:20-27`: means at bin midpoints; rows with no herd are skipped (`if sum(h) and sum(W)`). So a window's
gap is conditional on a herd existing, and a herd that died and refounded is averaged over its living years only. A run
with no herd row in the whole window is scored as gap 0 by corner_tab (the analysis session's uncommitted note, 2026-11-20,
says none occurred in exp005/exp006). Say both in Methods; they matter for the small-herd arms.

### 20. The syndrome definition is wide enough that "syndrome without pleiotropy" cannot fail
Draft introduction (draft.tex 72-73): "a set of traits that change together in the domesticated population relative to the
wild population, whatever the cause." Outline 375: "Lord et al.'s definition excludes our mechanism by construction."
Under this definition the wild moving away (finding 2) and the herd/no-herd mixture (finding 3) both count as a
syndrome. State what result would have counted against the claim.

### 21. The sex-cull preference weights are free parameters
buckPrefValue / doePrefValue are in meat units with no calibration (MODEL.md 372-378). The rules block "runs rule ×
value" (batch1-plan 14-16). Pre-register the values and why, and include value 0 as the control alongside the random
rule.

## Questions (not anchored well enough to be findings)

- **Q1.** Is the bistability (outline 28-29) the product of the load rule and a fixed capture inflow? A small herd's R
  is diluted by reactive captives, which are a large share of it, while its filter is weak (load near minLoad); a large
  herd's captives are a small share and its filter is strong. That predicts two stable states. The `herdPerHerder=1e9`
  arm in finding 1 would test it.
- **Q2.** Why does removing buck management take A from -0.15 to -0.49 (EXPLOG 5285, 5292)? Contest injury in dense pens
  is my guess; the paper should not interpret A's largest effect without a mechanism measured.
- **Q3.** Does the herd reproduce the Daly et al. pattern (narrow Y, diverse mtDNA)? Outline 371-372: "In results/gentrace
  H5 the two lines were similarly deep", on the old model. If v0.1.0 also gives similar depths, the paper's own framing
  (sires carry the isolation) is not what the model shows.
- **Q4.** What do model years mean? Herding starts at year 400; jumps come at 800 to after 4000 (EXPLOG 5326-5361); B is
  still moving 3,600 years after herding began. The archaeology has size change 500-1,000 years after management
  (outline 78). If model years are years, the model's size change is several times too slow; if they are not, the paper
  can claim order only (outline Q8 is still open).

## What I would want answered before the ~370 runs launch
In order of cost: findings 2(b) and 1(b) are two check designs (no-selection lineage control; fixed load) that decide
whether the lineage block and the R claims are worth running as planned. Finding 6's no-quota bottom corner is a handful
of runs. Finding 9 needs a seed cap for B4 written into its pre-registration. Findings 2(a), 7, 10 and 11 need analysis
tools, not runs.
