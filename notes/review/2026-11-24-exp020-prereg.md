**The code.**

- **Origin.** Origin cannot change while a goat is in the herd:

  - exchange is off.
- **No draws.** The midday sample calls only walksWithHerder, isSheltered, isKeptAtCamp and herdLoad.
- **Spooks.** Only walking goats can be spooked (animals.js:272-286 routes led, kept and kid goats away from

**The R accounting in measure 2 is an exact identity.** With R̄₀ = S₀/N₀, the year's change is
S₁/N₁ − R̄₀ = Σ_in (S_k − n_k R̄₀)/N₁ − Σ_out (S_k − n_k R̄₀)/N₁. That holds because a goat's R does not change, as the
closure confirms. One consequence matters for the readings: a goat that enters and leaves within the same year
contributes exactly zero.

## 1. Losses can be most negative through captive turnover alone; split every channel by origin (major)
A captive enters with a wild R, above the herd's R̄, so capture's contribution is positive. If the captive is then lost,
its loss contributes about as much negatively: at once if in the same year, otherwise one or a few years later, against
a slightly different R̄. In the early herd this is most of the traffic. Over the probe's herding years (401-470), ledger_check
prints 312 captures and 381 escapes (277 on refetch).

So "losses are the most negative channel" (P2) can hold through captives arriving and leaving again. That is not a
filter acting on the herd's own lineage, and it is not the R gap Chris asked about. The ledger records origin for every
flow, but measure 2 sums the channels without it.

**Fix:**
- Measure 2 prints each channel's contribution by origin: captured, herd-born, and both together.
- P2 says which it is read on. I'd register it on both: losses most negative overall, and herd-born losses most negative
  among herd-born channels, each in 6 or more of 8.
- If P2 is read only on all origins, the reading for Chris must say that captive turnover is in it.

**Name the competitors now, so the "if P2 fails" sentence has readings ready.**
- **Herd-sire births** (buckManagement 1, sireRule 'oldest', keepSires 2; world.js:1044-1062). The two oldest bucks
  sire. They are the bucks that have survived the filter longest, so herd-sire births may enter below R̄. In this
  identity that is selection by fecundity and sire choice, not a "spook" channel.
- **Slaughter.** In REF the cull has no flightiness term: flightCost 0 and nuisanceCost 0 (params.js:365, :273), as
  in the job list. So any R contribution from slaughter comes through age, sex, size or kidding record.

Say both in the entry, as candidates and unchecked.

## 2. Phase B is not the take-off century (major)
J is jump_tab's establishment year. That is the first year at which the trailing 100-year mean of the herd is 100 or
more, and stays there for 400 years (jump_tab.py:23-29 and :49-55). So by J the raw herd has been climbing for up to a
century, and the climb Chris asked to see ("the period when the R moves and the herd grows") falls mostly at the end
of phase A, not in B. Phase A as registered joins two different periods:
- REF's long small-herd stretch (herd 12-66 over J−400..J−100; exp010 jump_herd.txt lines 64-71);
- the take-off itself.

LOAD05 is worse. Its J sits at or near the tool's floor of 501, the first year it can report:
- s4 and s6 are at 501, s3 at 507, and s1-s8 span 501-669 (exp010 jump_herd.txt lines 16-23);
- so LOAD05's take-off lies within 401-500, its phase A is about a century long, and its J is left-censored.

**Fix:**
- Split phase A at J−100, so there are four phases:
  - A = 401..J−100, the small herd;
  - T = J−99..J, the smoothing window, where the herd climbs;
  - B = J+1..J+100;
  - C = J+101..1600.
- Read P2 over A, T and B separately as well as pooled.
- Say that LOAD05's J is at the floor in two seeds and at 531 or earlier in six, so its T marks the latest its herd can
  have taken off, not when it did.
- **Correct "no J by 1500".** jump_tab follows a run only to its last row minus the 400-year hold, which is 1200 here. A
  later crossing is printed as "late" and not counted. Write "no J by 1200". exp010's REF and LOAD05 Js are all at or
  before 957.

## 3. P1 is arithmetic on herd size (major)
With economy 0, a camp has exactly one herder whenever it has a herd: `herdN[c.id] = 1` (world.js:252). So
herdLoad = max(0.25, herdCount / 20) (world.js:229). "Realized load above 2" is therefore the same as "mean camp herd
above 40 goats".

That can already be read from exp018's REF records (`herded`, and herdComp per managed camp-day) before the batch runs.
It tests herd size, not a mechanism.

The contrast it relies on is also not like for like. "Every fixed load of 2 or more lost every herd in exp010" compares:
- a load of 2 or more applied from year 400 to newly captured, high-R goats;
- REF's high load, which arrives only after the herd has been through the filter.

What lets REF's herd survive at that load is what its walking goats are like by then. The ledger records exactly that,
as walk[o][3] / walk[o][0], the rule's daily rate per walking goat-day.

**Fix:**
- Keep P1 as a descriptive check, labelled as following from herdN = 1.
- If a prediction is wanted, predict the per-walking-goat rate instead. For example, REF's rate over phase B is at or
  below LOAD05's over its phase B in 6 or more of 8 seeds, on a set criterion (point 4).
- **Correct the wording.** exp010 shows that no LOAD2 or LOAD3 seed established: jump_herd.txt lines 9-10 print 0 of 8
  for each. Write that, not "lost every herd", unless a printed number shows herds that formed and were lost.

## 4. P3: the denominator, the pairing and undefined ratios (moderate)
- **The denominator.** "Lost / mean stock" uses year-end stock. A captive taken and lost within the same year is never
  in the stock, so captured goats' exposure is undercounted and their rate overstated. The midday sample already gives
  exposure by origin: walk + kept + other goat-days ÷ yearDays. Use that, and print the year-end-stock version beside it.
- **The pairing.** Pairing REF seed k with LOAD05 seed k is arbitrary. The two runs share a seed number but are
  different histories from year 400, the same point as my exp018 point 2. Use a set criterion, as exp018's P4 was
  amended: for example, REF's ratio is above LOAD05's median in 6 or more of 8 seeds.
- **Undefined cases.** Phase B may hold few captives. Register a floor (for example, at least N captured goat-years in
  the phase) and what happens to a seed below it or with no captured losses (ratio undefined: named, and counted as not
  read). Say whether "every seed" then means every seed that is read.
- **Timing.** LOAD05's Js are 501-669 and REF's 588-957 (exp010 jump_herd.txt), so the two arms' phase B fall in
  different centuries, with a wild that is itself changing. Say this beside P3. It is not a reason to change P3.
- **Kept goats.** Tethered captives (tetherNew 1; R × fear ≥ 0.35) do not walk and cannot run off (animals.js:272-274).
  Read captive attrition beside measure 4's kept share, so that a low captive loss rate is not read as captives being
  tame.

## 5. The midday sample is unchecked, and the analysis tool cannot be tested on this probe (moderate)
**ledger_check tests flows and events, not the `day` block,** although measures 1, 3 and 4 rest on it. There are free
checks that can fail:
- **Walking goats.** walk goat-days, summed over camps and origins, should equal density.c[1].n in the same year row.
  Both are taken at the same midday sub-tick, with the same walksWithHerder test (world.js:477, :493). kept and other
  should match density.c[2] and c[3], except on camps with no herder, which the ledger skips. The tool prints any
  difference.
- **Births by sire.** born and bornW are checked only as a sum. Check bornW against the row's wildSiredShare ×
  herdBirths, to its 3 decimal places. That catches a swap between the two channels.
- **Stock R against the histograms.** histPeriod 1 gives annual histograms. Print the stock's mean R beside histHerd's
  binned mean each year, since the R gap that goes to Chris is computed from the histograms.
- **Load.** Print the spooks per walking goat-day beside mean walk[3] / walk[0]. They should be close but not equal,
  because one is a midday snapshot and the other counts the whole day. A large gap would mean the spook count and the
  sample do not describe the same goats.

**The probe has no J.** Under the 400-year hold, the 470-year probe's follow-up ends before herding begins, so no J is

## C. P2(b) has to be read against the herd-born goats' own mean (major)
As written, each channel's contribution is (S_k − n_k R̄₀)/N, with R̄₀ the pooled stock's mean, captives included.
Wherever captives sit above the herd-born goats in R, as they enter with the wild's R, R̄₀ sits above the herd-born
mean. A herd-born goat can then be among the highest-R herd-born goats and still fall below R̄₀, and losing it shows as
a positive contribution. That is a property of the reference, not a reading: by how much the two origins differ is for
the batch to show.

So P2(b) can fail even when the filter is removing the herd-born line's highest-R goats. That is the reading (b) exists
to detect.

The herd-born stock closes on its own: ledger_check checks closure per origin. Its mean therefore splits exactly over its
own channels, with R̄₀ and N taken from the herd-born stock alone:
- in: born and bornW;
- out: the herd-born lost, culled and died.

**Fix:**
- Register (b) on that split.
- Have the tool check that its channels sum to the herd-born mean's change, per year and per phase, as it does for the
  pooled mean.
- Print the pooled-reference origin columns as well, since they are what sums to the pooled change. Say in one line
  which is which.
