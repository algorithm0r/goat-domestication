## 1. "Waited" drops seeds that are still waiting at the follow-up end, so the count falls at high loads by censoring (major)
Q1 labels a seed "waited" only if it then establishes by the follow-up end (load_tab.py:37-49; jump_tab's end is year
3599). A seed that sits in the small state past 3599 is "never". exp010's LOAD1 already shows this: s5 crossed only at
3708, after the follow-up end, and it is labelled "never (late 3708)" (scored.txt line 27). So LOAD1 counts 7, not 8.

The higher the load, the longer the wait, so more waiting seeds will be censored at 0.8-0.9. That breaks both readings:
- **The decision rule.** A level where all 8 seeds wait but 2 are still waiting at 3599 reads 6 of 8, which is
  "graded".
- **Prediction 2.** LOAD09 at 8 waited, against LOAD1's censored 7, fails "non-decreasing". Censoring causes that
  failure, not the process.

**Fix:**
- For the rule and prediction 2, count a seed as waited if its 100-year herd mean stays under 60 for 300+ consecutive
  years after 500, whether or not it establishes by 3599. Print the two parts: waited then established, and still
  waiting at the end.
- Recompute LOAD05 and LOAD1 with the same tool line. LOAD1 is then 8 of 8 if s5's small state meets the span; the
  tool decides.
- exp010's registered count stands for exp010.

## 2. The 300-year span turns a continuous delay into a cut at about year 800 (major, for your (1))
The first testable point is year 501, so "waited" needs a jump after about year 800. That makes "waited" nearly
"established after about 800 from a small herd". At LOAD05 every J is 501-669 (jump_herd.txt), and at LOAD1 978-3171.
The new levels will put J between those, right where the 300-year cut sits. If the wait shortens smoothly with load, a
level whose seeds jump at 700-900 splits into grew and waited by where each falls relative to 800. Then the count's
switch or grade partly reflects the cut. The 60-goat ceiling has the same exposure if the plateau rises as the load
falls (LOAD1's plateaus were 5-12).

**Fix (pre-registered beside the rule, not replacing it):**
- Per seed, print J, the longest span under 60 after 500, and the median herd over that span.
- Print the waited count (point 1's definition) at spans of 200 and 400 years as well as 300.
- If the rule's reading (switch or graded) changes between 200 and 400, label it cut-dependent and lead with the per-seed J
  distribution (KM curves) instead of the counts.

## 3. The 0-1 / 7-8 cut cannot tell a switch from a gradient with one intermediate level (moderate, for your (1))
With 8 seeds a count carries wide binomial noise. The exact 95% intervals are 0.03-0.65 for 2 of 8 and 0.35-0.97 for
6 of 8. The rule turns on a single level, though: one level at 2-6 makes the result "graded". But a steep transition
whose midpoint falls near one of the four levels gives exactly that. And a gradual one can give all-extreme counts if
its width is under the 0.1 spacing. So the data can resolve where the transition is and an upper bound on its width,
not "switch versus graded" as such. The rule also has no reading for a non-monotone pattern (for example 0, 8, 1, 8).

**Fix:** state the readings in terms of resolution, with each level's exact binomial interval printed.
- **No level at 2-6, counts in load order:** "the wait switches on between levels a and b; the transition is narrower
  than 0.1 in load at 8 seeds".
- **Exactly one level at 2-6:** "the transition lies at about that level; its width is not resolved".
- **Two or more levels at 2-6:** "graded over at least 0.1-0.2 of load". This matches prediction 2's second half.
- **Counts not in load order (a fall of 2 or more between neighbours):** "no single threshold; reported per seed".

## 4. Prediction 3: ties pass as worded; the median needs a floor, an undefined case and its convention (minor, your (2))
"Non-decreasing" admits ties, as exp010's clarification (f2555ff) read breadth. Say so. The real gaps are elsewhere:
- **The floor.** J cannot be earlier than the first testable point (about 501; LOADLO's KM median is 501, LOAD05's 525).
  Equal medians at 0.5-0.6 can be a floor, not equal times. A tie there passes, but say it carries no information.
- **An undefined median.** If fewer than half the seeds establish by 3599, jump_tab prints no median (line 130). Read
  such a median as above every defined one. Two undefined medians tie and pass.
- **The convention.** jump_tab's median is the first event year at which the KM fraction reaches 0.5, not load_tab's
  upper median of established years. The prediction should name it, since the two differ (exp010: 525 against 528).

With point 1's still-waiting seeds censored at 3599, the KM median already handles them correctly. It is the count in
point 1 that doesn't.

## 5. The R gap at high loads is shortened by late establishment, and prediction 4 doesn't say how it compares (moderate)
In exp010's LOAD1 the late seeds had the smallest gaps: s5 −0.056, s6 −0.127, s8 −0.127 (J 3171, 3039; the flag
paragraph). A seed that establishes late has had less time as a herd before the window. So at 0.8-0.9 the filter pushes
the gap down while later establishment pulls it up. "LOAD09 more negative than LOAD06" tests the sum of the two.

**Fix:**
- Say how prediction 4 compares the levels: arm means over established seeds (load_tab's "R est"). Name it, since a
  paired interval would read differently.
- Beside it, as descriptive lines: per seed, the R gap with J (or years established before 3201), and herd change and
  wild change against the founders (split_tab, established seeds) per level. Fixed-load herds are open, and the wild
  follows them (exp010: wild herd-born share 0.72-0.89), so the gap alone understates the herd's move. That is the same
  caution as SCHED in exp014.

The registered descriptive line, waited against not-waited R gaps, does part of this. J does the rest without a new
category.
