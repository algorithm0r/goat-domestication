# Review of the exp014 pre-registration (2026-11-22, before any main run)

On EXPLOG "## exp014" (536bc53); the switches in `src/animals.js` (spook chance; f8848f0, c72d86f) and `World.spookAt`;
and tools/runoff_rate.py.

The switches do what the entry says:
- With spookSchedule set, every herd goat's daily spook chance is `spookAt()`, with no R, fearOfHumans or herdLoad.
  Recapture is untouched.
- The identity checks (0 of 266) cover both switches when off.

Points 1-3 should be settled before the main runs. Point 1 also bears on the calibration check now running.

## 1. The calibration check reads more than run-off rates (major for your question 3)
runoff_rate.py prints, per file: rows, **mean herd**, ranOff, refetched and **escaped**. Two of those are outcomes:
- Mean herd is close to establishment (prediction 1).
- Escaped is the loss rate, which is the filter the decision rule is about.

The scratch runs are SCHED seeds 1-2 with the main arguments. The sim is deterministic, so their 1600 years are the
first 1600 years of the main SCHED seeds 1 and 2. Whatever the check prints has been read from the main runs.

**Fix:** before the check is read, give runoff_rate a flag that prints ranOff only, with the per-period pass or fail.
Alternatively, have someone with no stake run it and report only that. If the output has already been read with herd
and escaped visible, record it in the entry as the reviewer's exp010 provenance note did.

A second point: on a failure, the divisor is rescaled from SCHED's own run-off rate. That is calibration of the
manipulation, so it's legitimate. Keep it to ranOff alone, and record each attempt's ranOff in the amendment.

## 2. SCHEDT − REFT changes four things, and branch 2 names one (major)
SCHEDT differs from REFT in four ways:
- **R in the spook,** which branch 2 names.
- **fearOfHumans in the spook.** Captives carry high fear, and home-born goats low.
- **Per-herd load.** The schedule replays REF's average time course, not each herd's own load.
- **The run-off rate level.** The schedule matches REF's curve, not REFT's. REFT is a new arm: a closed herd with
  falling R will run off less than REF's, so SCHEDT may get more run-offs than its contrast. More run-offs means more
  recapture tests, which would bias SCHEDT's R gap toward REFT's.

So "the filter needs R on the run-off side (run-offs concentrated on reactive goats)" over-attributes.

**Fix:**
- Word branch 2 as "needs the run-off side to read the goat (R × fearOfHumans) and its herd's load".
- Register that REFT's and SCHEDT's achieved run-off rates per century are reported side by side. If they differ by
  more than 15% in the window, the branch reading is labelled as also carrying a rate difference.
- REFT remains the right paired contrast for SCHEDT: same seeds, same closure rule, same recapture. There is no cleaner
  one without a schedule calibrated to REFT, which can't exist before REFT runs.

## 3. Branch 1 claims more than the schedule allows (moderate)
"Neither R on the run-off side nor herd-size scaling is needed" isn't licensed on scaling. The schedule replays REF's
run-off curve by time, and that curve was produced by REF's load rising with herd size (centuries 2-4 peak at 2.3, then
fall). The scaling's average effect is in the schedule. What SCHEDT removes is the per-herd dependence.

**Fix:** "neither R on the run-off side nor a per-herd load is needed; the run-off time course is REF's".

## 4. Outcomes the rule doesn't cover (moderate)
- **REFT's R gap above −0.30.** Closing REF with the taper may change its filter: captive inflow stops, and with it the
  reactive goats the filter removes. Branch 2 then can't be read, and prediction 2's comparison loses its reference.
  State the reading: "REF's R filter does not survive closure by taper; SCHEDT is read against 0 only".
- **SCHEDT established but not closed.** The taper needs selfDoes 10 home-born breeding does. With run-offs spread over
  every goat, calm home-born does are lost too, and a herd may establish without closing. Add closure (captive-sire
  share under 0.05 in 6 or more of 8, as exp013) as a condition of branches 1 and 2. If it fails, SCHEDT is read as an
  open herd.
- **SCHED (open) has predictions but no branch.** Say what SCHED − REF (paired) is for. As I read it, it's the open-herd
  version of the same question. Read it beside the rule, with the exp010 caution: in an open herd the wild follows, so
  report herd change against the founders beside the gap.

## 5. Minor
- **A zero in the schedule would silently restore REF's rule.** `sf > 0 ? sf / spookSteps : (herderAlarm × R × fear ×
  load)` switches back to the R-based spook whenever the schedule value is 0. The current schedule has no zero, so the
  runs are fine. A guard (an error on a zero or negative entry) would stop a later schedule from mixing rules
  unannounced.
- **The divisor 240 comes from probes whose herds collapsed to 1-2 goats.** Their exposure (penned, tethered, at heel)
  needn't match a herd of 250. The calibration check exists for this, and it is the right step.
- **The burn-in (exp013) doesn't bear on the R gap.** R's wild change after 400 is small in every arm (−0.05 to
  −0.08), so herding at 400 is fine for this question.

## On the amendment (3dfc9bb), before any main run
It answers points 1-4. `runoff_rate.py --check` prints only mean ranOff per period, target and pass/fail (its one leak,
"no herd goats in any file", is unavoidable and fine). The guard in `spookAt` throws on a value of 0 or below; it changes
nothing for a valid schedule, so no identity check is needed, but the main runs will carry 3dfc9bb or later rather than
the registered c72d86f, and the raw entry should say so. Branches 1 and 2 are worded as asked, closure is a condition,
and the uncovered outcomes have readings. Accepted.
