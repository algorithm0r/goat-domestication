# Review of the exp013 pre-registration (2026-11-21, before any run)

On EXPLOG "## exp013" (9522ce9) and tools/exp013.jsonl (24 runs). The jobs are their year-400 arms' arguments plus
humansAt 2900, herdingAt 3000 and ibdFrom 3000; I checked the first job of each arm against exp011's and exp005's
argument lists. Moving herding past the wild's settling is the right test, and 2900 is a safe choice over the 2000 I
proposed. Points 1 and 2 should be fixed by amendment before any run starts.

## 1. The third branch misreads its own outcome, and the design lacks the control that would read it (major)
The year-400 arms' wild change after 400 can have two causes:
- **(a) The wild still settling under wolves after the burn-in.** This is my 8fee17d reading.
- **(b) Humans themselves:** hunting from year 300, and herding's effects on the wild from 400.

The late arms remove (a) but keep (b). So:
- If prediction 1 holds (the wild is settled before 2900) and prediction 2 fails (the wild still moves 0.05 or more
  after 3000), the wild's change is caused by humans, not by the burn-in. The third branch reads that outcome as "the
  wild is not settled by 2900", which prediction 1 will just have contradicted.
- Under (b), a closed herd's A, S, H and W gaps are the herd sheltered from selection that humans put on the wild.
  That would be a model result, not burn-in residue, and the year-400 arms' gaps would partly stand.

**The control: WTlate.** It has the same arguments as REFlate with humansAt 1e9, runs 6600 years and uses seeds 1-8.
It shares each seed's history to 2900 with the three late arms, so the late arms' wild change minus WTlate's, paired by
seed, is the wild change that humans cause. It also measures the no-human drift over the same 3600 years, which is the
reference prediction 2's 0.05 bar needs (point 4). This control matters more than FLATlate.

**Fix (amendment):**
- Add WTlate.
- Restate prediction 2 as the paired wild change, late arm minus WTlate, on A, S, H and W.
- Give the rule a branch for each outcome:
  - **Prediction 1 fails:** the settling time goes to Chris.
  - **Prediction 1 holds and the paired human-caused wild change is under 0.05:** branches 1 and 2 as written.
  - **Prediction 1 holds and the human-caused wild change is 0.05 or more:** the wild moves because humans arrive.
    The closed herds' A, S, H and W gaps are shelter from that, and they are read as herd isolation from human-driven
    selection on the wild, not as burn-in.
- Report, per trait, the year-400 arms' wild change against the late arms'. The difference is the burn-in's share.

## 2. The rule doesn't read the four traits that motivated the batch (major)
Branch 1 fires on "closed late breadth below 3". Late breadth can fall below 3 for reasons other than the A, S, H and W
gaps vanishing. For example, T's or B's herd change could be smaller from a settled founder pool. A breadth of 3 or
more could also be reached by traits other than these four. The question asked was whether these four gaps are
burn-in.

**Fix (amendment):**
- **The primary reading:** per arm (REFlate, LARDlate, TAPERlate) and per trait (A, S, H, W), the late gap against
  the year-400 arm's gap. This is an unpaired 8-against-8 difference with a 95% interval, since the histories differ
  from year 0 to 300.
- **The prediction, if the burn-in reading is right:** each late gap is under 0.10 in size, and the interval of the
  difference excludes 0.
- **Breadth:** name which breadth governs branches 1 and 2. Plain breadth, as amendment 8 of exp011 has it, or
  herd-carried breadth (culture_tab 48a8de9)? Branch 2's "3 or more with herd-carried gaps" reads like the second. If
  so, say "herd-carried breadth, by per-seed vote", and say what a "mixed" vote reads as. The vote is already mixed in
  the year-400 LARD and TAPER (4 and 5 of 8).
- **When LARDlate and TAPERlate disagree:** say which reading follows.
- **REFlate:** prediction 4 covers R only. Add A and S, whose gaps are the wild-carried ones in REF.

## 4. The bars (your question 2) (moderate)
**Prediction 1's 0.02:**
- "Stays within 0.02 of its 2801-2900 mean from century 2701" tests only two centuries.
- At k = 5 (runyears thinning for 6600 years), each century holds 4 histogram rows. It holds 6-7 at 4000 years.
- Per-century noise in the wild mean is not in the record. WT moved up to 0.034 (L) and 0.030 (S) between 2901-3000
  and 3901-4000 (4 seeds).
- **Fix:** read it on seed means, and compare 2401-2600 with 2701-2900 (two 200-year means) within 0.02. Alternatively,
  WTlate gives the drift directly (point 1).

**Prediction 2's 0.05:** the bar is reasonable against 0.08-0.25, but it should be read against WTlate's no-human
drift over the same span, not against 0. WT's S moved 0.030 in 1000 years with no humans. Over 3600 years, drift alone
could approach 0.05 on S.

**The entry's "an old-model no-human run settled only by about year 2900"** overstates the record. WT was within 0.05
on every core trait from 1501-1600 to 3901-4000 (the largest is L at 0.049). 2900 was the window the prediction chose,
not the first settled one. Herding at 3000 is still the safe choice.

## 5. FLATlate (your question 4) (minor)
FLATlate is not needed for the question. In the year-400 arms, the wild's change is the same whether the herd is open
or closed (8fee17d's table). An open herd tracks it, and nothing suggests the late world changes that. WTlate (point
1) is the control the reading depends on. Add FLATlate only if the queue has room after WTlate.

## 6. Minor
- The year-400 arms recorded at k = 3 and the late arms will record at k = 5. Windows and founders are therefore
  averaged over different numbers of rows. State this. No bias is expected, since each is the mean of its window.
- **The note on exp011 that goes with this:** the analysis session's reading of the code (world.js:129, 1487-1496,
  1626-1654) shows the selection pool is taken at day 0 of the birth year, after most conceptions. So most captive
  sires are in the pool. My 8fee17d point 3 rests instead on sires that leave between conception and day 0 (culled,
