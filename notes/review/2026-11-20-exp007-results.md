# Review of exp007's results (2026-11-20)

On EXPLOG "exp007 results (raw)" and "(scored)" (c4a6253, 3c6ff20), results/exp007_design-checks/scored.txt, and
flow_3201-4000.txt (tools/flow_tab.py, exploratory). The scoring follows the registered rules and amendments as
written, and the predictions are scored correctly. What follows is about what the results mean for the paper, plus one
error of mine in the amendment rule.

## 1. Without the size-scaled spook load, the whole divergence goes, not only R (fatal to the current framing)
LOADLO, which is REF with the spook load fixed at minLoad, keeps a herd of 262 against REF's 288. Every core gap is
within 0.04 of zero, and breadth is 0 against REF's 6. B is -0.009 against -0.295, although the keep value's
growth credit and the oldest-sire rule are both still on. T is +0.039 against +0.447. So the load rule
(`world.js:181-183`, used only at `animals.js:363`) is a precondition for every trait gap in REF, not just for R.

The flow table shows how, though the reading is mine and the tool is exploratory. Captures per herd goat are about the
same: 0.030 in LOADLO, 0.026 in REF. The captives' share of breeding is not:
- conceptions by captured sires: 0.288 against 0.064;
- conceptions by captured dams: 0.096 against 0.032;
- herd depth: 4.66 against 14.69 generations;
- the wild genome's share from herd-born goats: 0.886 against 0.128, with escape rates alike (0.074 against 0.082).

The barrier in REF works in both directions. Reactive captives leave the herd before they breed, and herd-born escapees
add little to the wild. Both depend on the herd-wild R difference, and the load-scaled spook filter is what makes it
(LOADLO R gap -0.055). In speciation terms this is selection against migrants (immigrant inviability), with R as the
trait it acts on.

**What follows for the paper.**
- The model's "isolation that survives gene flow" is a filter on immigrants built by one assumed rule: spooking
  proportional to R × fear × herd/20 per herder. It is not isolation by sire choice. NOCULL makes the same point from
  the other side: no cull, no husbandry and no buck management, yet depth 17.05, captured sires 0.030, and R -0.645.
- That may be a better thesis than the current one, since tameness as the barrier is testable and has a literature.
  It stands or falls on herdLoad, though, so the paper needs:
  - an empirical argument for spook rate rising with flock size per herder;
  - a dose-response sweep of `herderAlarm` and `herdPerHerder`, reporting depth, the flow measures and the gap vector;
  - the gene-flow numbers above as main-text results, by a registered measure.
- The lineage-isolation claim (contribution 1) and the "map from behaviour to gene" (contribution 3) are both
  conditional on this filter being on. Every behaviour's effect in exp005/exp006 was measured with it on.

## 2. My between-arm "carried by" rule could not detect what it was written for (my error)
In amendment 2, as I asked for it, "carried by" splits CTLDEEP − CTLRAND into herd change and wild change. The two
arms share a seed-determined wild: wild-change differences are at most 0.026 (scored table), and same-seed r for wild
change is 0.5-0.8 (the analysis session's tools/pair_tab.py). So every between-arm difference reads as "herd change"
by construction. The analysis session's within-arm split is the right instrument. It shows CTLRAND and CTLSHAL track
the wild on every trait (herd change within 0.025 of wild change). CTLDEEP's S (herd -0.027, wild -0.263), W
(+0.085 / +0.222) and A (-0.092 / +0.106) are mostly the wild moving while the deep herd stays. So design-review
finding 2(b) holds for S, W and A. The registered reading of prediction 3 ("all six carried by herd change") should
carry a note in EXPLOG that the rule was uninformative for this comparison. The within-arm split should be the
registered instrument from exp008 on.

## 3. Hypothesis: the deep herd's own changes may be the mutation model regressing toward 0.5 (major if confirmed)
Mutation moves every allele U(±0.04) at every meiosis and clamps it to [0, 1] (`genome.js:11`, `params.js:383`).
Any bounded, unbiased mutation has a stationary mean of 0.5. Under relaxed selection, a trait's mean therefore drifts
toward 0.5, at a rate set by mutationStep. My arithmetic: the per-generation step variance is 0.08²/12 ≈ 5.3e-4, so
over about 1,000 generations an allele spreads with SD about 0.7. That is enough to flatten the distribution toward
uniform.

In CTLDEEP the traits where the herd moves *against* the wild all move toward 0.5, if the founders sit near the
pre-batch-1 wild means (MODEL §9: A 0.74, H 0.80, B 0.89, T 0.44; wild R about 0.86 by year 400, MODEL G13):
- A: herd -0.092 while the wild went +0.106;
- H: -0.115 against +0.081;
- B: -0.079 against +0.018;
- T: +0.134 against -0.021;
- R: -0.224 against -0.059.

The neutral marker C cannot detect this. It starts as U[0, 1] alleles, already at the stationary mean, so "C's
difference as the noise reference" is a null for drift, not for mutational regression. If this is right, part of what
an isolated herd shows is relaxed selection plus a mutation model whose bias points to the middle of an arbitrary
[0, 1] scale. Traits whose wild mean is above 0.5 (R, A, B, H, W) then fall, which looks like the syndrome. The
direction is an artefact of the scale. Real mutation biases on quantitative traits exist but have no reason to point at
0.5.

**What would settle it** (cheap first):
- (a) From split_tab's founders' means F (the tool computes them; print them): for every trait in CTLDEEP, compare
  sign(0.5 − F) with the sign of the herd's own change, and compare the size with the diffusion expectation.
- (b) One arm, CTLDEEP with `mutationStep` 0.02. If the deep herd's own changes are mostly mutational, they should
  shrink by roughly the square of the step ratio over the same years; selection-driven changes should not.
- (c) If confirmed, every isolated-herd gap needs the mutational null beside it. The paper must say which gaps exceed
  it.

- **Bistability (design review Q1).** By herd size, LOADLO and all three CTL arms establish at once (left-censored at
  501). In exp006, RAND and noProv sat at 20-50 goats for thousands of years. That suggests the long small state comes

  a registered comparison.
- **NOCULL** (8 of 8, herd 207): the outline banner's "from the bare culture only the threshold cull starts a herd" is
  wrong. It was the 13% quota. NOCULL's A gap -0.585, with no buck management, matches noBuck's -0.491. The A mechanism

  the scoring says so correctly.
- **Identity check:** it did not run with the batch, because its job list was written to /tmp. Until it reports, the
  REF comparison rests on the code-diff argument in the raw entry (empty diff over src, load.mjs and runyears.mjs).
  That argument is strong, but it is not the check that was registered.

Section 1 says "herd-born escapees add little to the wild". The route is not established. In the CTL arms escapes are 0,

## Section 3, after the founders' sign test (analysis session, split_founders.txt)
Founders (301-400), identical across arms by shared seeds: R 0.791, A 0.729, S 0.413, B 0.873, H 0.740, T 0.184,
M 0.438, W 0.538, L 0.418, C 0.522. CTLDEEP's herd change points toward 0.5 on every trait whose founders sit far from
0.5: R, A, B, H, T. On the four that sit near 0.5 (S, M, W, L) it does not, and there the pull would be weak anyway.
The sign test is consistent with the hypothesis but does not decide it. The analysis session's caution is right:
selection in the herd's own environment pointing the same way would give the same signs.

**The mutationStep 0.02 arm is weaker than I said.** Under bounded, unbiased mutation the endpoint is 0.5 whatever the
step. The step sets only the pace, with diffusion time scaling as 1/step². So at 0.02 a mutational pull should be
about four times slower over the same years. But CTLDEEP's IBD is high (depth 19.6). With standing variance used up, a
selection response also waits on new mutation, so it slows at 0.02 too. Halving the step therefore slows both
explanations, and a smaller change at 0.02 would not separate them.

**A cleaner test:** a neutral marker that starts far from 0.5. C is read by nothing (MODEL C16) and draws from its own
random stream (`genome.js:36`, rand3). If C's initial alleles were drawn from, say, U[0.6, 1.0] (mean 0.8) instead of
U[0, 1], the herd and wild C would show the pure mutational pull at each arm's own depth and Ne, with no selection
possible. Compare CTLDEEP's herd change on A, B and H (founders 0.73-0.87) with the shifted C's herd change over the
same years. If they move at about the same rate, the "own change" is mutational. If A, B and H move clearly faster, it
is selection. This needs a small code switch (the steward's call). The same number of rand3 draws keeps every other
stream identical, so it costs one identity check and one CTLDEEP/CTLRAND pair.

**Prediction**, if the deep herd's own change is mutational: a shifted C (initial alleles U[0.6, 1.0], scored from its own
301-400 F) reaches a fraction of 0.3-0.6 in CTLDEEP over 3201-4000, with A, H and T inside its 95% interval, and near 0
in CTLRAND. If C's fraction is below 0.15 while A, H and T stay near 0.4-0.5, the deep herd's own change is selection,
not mutational pull. Between 0.15 and 0.3 I'd call it unresolved at 8 seeds. Caveat from the analysis session: the
pace depends on how many alleles sit near a bound, and no saved record holds alleles. The genotype SD, wider for C
