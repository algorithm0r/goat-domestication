# Review of the exp011 pre-registration (2026-11-20, before results)

On EXPLOG "## exp011" (65e8cc0), code aab0e86 (params.js, world.js, MODEL C57 "Herder-culture rules"), and
tools/exp011.jsonl. I read every changed line. The code does what the entry says. `runOffLoss` 0 guards all three loss
paths: refetch (world.js:769), the keepers' chase (794) and the night return (858). `captureTaper` covers live
capture (653, 663) and both orphan paths (693, 724). The jobs are REF plus `herdPerHerder=1e9 minLoad=1 runOffLoss=0`,
with each arm's switch. FLAT differs from exp010's LOAD1 only in `runOffLoss` (both log histDepth by default).

Three majors, all of which bear on how the decision rule reads, and four smaller points. The steward asked about the
"closed" and "diverged" definitions (points 1 and 4) and about selfDoes = 10 (point 5).

## 1. "Diverged" can be met by a closed herd under no selection at all (major)
"Diverged" is breadth 3 or more: core traits with |seed-mean gap| ≥ 0.10 (tools/exp007_tab.py:8). A closed herd moves
on two routes that no herder rule drives, and exp007 measured both in CTLDEEP:
- **The wild moves while the herd stays.** S (herd −0.027, wild −0.263) and W (+0.085, +0.222) were mostly wild change.
- **The herd's own change points toward 0.5** on every trait whose founders sit far from it. exp009 is testing whether
  that is the mutational pull. Founders' fractions of (0.5 − F) times the founders' distances give:
  - R 0.77 × (0.5 − 0.791) = −0.22;
  - H 0.48 × (0.5 − 0.740) = −0.12;
  - T 0.43 × (0.5 − 0.184) = +0.14;
  - A 0.40 × (0.5 − 0.729) = −0.09;
  - B 0.21 × (0.5 − 0.873) = −0.08.

So R, H and T alone pass 0.10. A TAPER herd, closed by stopping capture with nothing in the herd selected, can reach
breadth 3 that way. If exp009 confirms the pull, branch 1 would then report the mutation model's bias toward the middle
of [0, 1] as divergence produced by herder culture. Prediction 2 expects the R gap above −0.30, and the pull alone
predicts −0.22.

**Fix (amendment, before results):**
- Count a trait toward "diverged" only if two things hold:
  - its herd change (the within-arm split, the registered instrument since exp008) exceeds the mutational expectation;
  - its herd change is larger than its wild change.
  The mutational expectation is the pooled C fraction × (0.5 − F), using exp009's pooled C fraction, or the run's own
  shifted C (below).
- Better and free: add `cInitLo=0.6` to all 48 jobs. C is read by nothing (MODEL C16), and the switch draws the same
  rand3 values (92d8f21, identity-checked), so every other outcome is unchanged. Each exp011 run then carries its own
  pull reference at its own depth and Ne, which pooling from exp009 cannot give.
- State branch 1 as conditional on the exp009 outcome. If exp009 reads "mutational", traits within the pull's reach
  don't count toward divergence.

## 2. The nuisance cull reads reactivity: the circularity returns with one step between (major for the framing)
`seen[0]` counts run-offs, and run-offs are produced by the spook line: chance herderAlarm × R × fearOfHumans × load
(animals.js:363), here at load 1. NUIS charges the keep value in proportion to that count (world.js:1248-1250). The
paper would then say: "we built a rule where herders cull the goats that run off, and our claim is that herding selects
against reactivity." That is the sentence Chris objected to, with a cultural label on the rule.

It is not fatal. A herder who eats the animal that costs him chasing is ethnographically defensible in a way that
"spook chance scales with herd size per herder" is not. The dose is in meat units per run-off, which can be compared
with a kid's worth. But the result to report is the dose at which R diverges and whether that cost is plausible. An R
gap under NUIS is not evidence that culture produces tameness. It shows that this charge, at this size, selects on R
by construction.

Two consequences for the entry:
- **Branch 3 is mis-stated.** "the spook filter can be retired from the reference culture in favour of it" — the spook
  line still generates the run-offs NUIS reads. What NUIS replaces is the loss consequence and the herd-size scaling,
  not the spook rule. Say that.
- **NUIS is partly a closure rule.** A captive's fearOfHumans starts high and habituates only at 0.02 a night outside
  the socialisation window (C58). A home-born goat sits near 0.05 from kidhood. In their first months captives run off
  many times as often, so NUIS eats captives first, as larder does, through fear rather than R. An R gap under NUIS
  could then come from closure (with the pull) rather than from selection on R among home-born goats. To separate the
  two, register the breeders' selection differential on R, `ys.selSire` / `ys.selDam` (parent minus the camp's sex
  mean, world.js:1477-1495), per arm:
  - NUIS selecting on R gives a negative R differential beyond FLAT's;
  - LARD and TAPER should show none.

**On the dose:** at 0.3 per run-off and about 1.5 run-offs a year, the charge is about 0.45 per remaining year.
kidValue is 0.3, so a doe kidding about once a year is credited about 0.3 a year. NUIS30 can make an average goat
worth less than its keep, so it may not establish at all. The decision rule has no branch for that (point 6).

## 3. FLAT is not shown to be R-neutral, and the decision rule reads R against 0 (major)
`runOffLoss` 0 removes the loss, not the run-off. Under refetchRunOffs 2, a bolted goat moves as a wild goat until a
keeper reaches it or night brings it back (world.js:776-797, 852-859). While loose it may be exposed to wolves and to
wild bucks, and it may miss grazing. Those are consequences in proportion to R × fear, the same as the run-off.
Prediction 1 tests this ("R gap above −0.20"). But branch 1 asks for "R near the wild", and prediction 2 reads the R gap
against 0, not against FLAT.

**Fix:**
- Read every arm's R gap, and the breadth traits, as the paired difference from FLAT by seed.
- Register FLAT − exp010 LOAD1 by seed. The two differ only in runOffLoss, so this is the cleanest test in either batch
  of whether loss is what makes the spook rule a filter. It is free, since both batches are already queued.
- Question: is there a counter for herd goats that die, or conceive by a wild sire, while loose? If not, FLAT's
  residual R selection has no measured route.

## 4. "Closed": a fair line, but placed in a gap no run has filled (moderate)
depth is the mean generations since wild of the herd genome (flow_tab.py:13). Under steady inflow m per generation it
sits near 1/m. Under complete closure it grows with the time since closure. In numbers:
- REF has depth 14.69 and captive-sire share 0.064, so REF counts as closed under this rule, which is right because REF
  diverged.
- LOADLO has 4.66 and 0.288.
- The threshold of 10 and 0.10 sits between them, where no run has landed, so nothing yet shows that divergence starts
  at 10.

**Fixes:**
- (a) Say in the entry that REF is closed by this definition.
- (b) Classify each established seed as closed or not and report counts, not arm means. These herds have shown
  bistable founding, and a mixture of closed and open seeds can average to about 10.
- (c) Branch 2 ("closed but not diverged: closure is necessary but not sufficient") needs closure matched to REF's.
  For example, a LARD herd at depth 11 that does not diverge while REF diverges at 14.7 shows only that 11 is not
  enough, not that closure is insufficient. Require depth at or above REF's for that branch. Otherwise read divergence
  against depth across exp007, exp010 and exp011 together. exp010's fixed loads will put herds in the 5-14 range.
- (d) TAPER passes "closed" trivially once captures stop, since depth then only measures time. That is fine, but its
  closure is then a design fact, not a result.

The AND with captive-sire share adds little to depth, which already counts dams (cdam) and any wild sire. I would keep
it, as the measure a reader understands.

## 5. selfDoes = 10 (moderate; my view, since it was asked)
- **The scored window probably won't depend on it.** In the probes herds of about 90 per camp held about 40 home-born
  breeding does. 10 then binds only during founding and after crashes. Where it matters is TAPER's founder pool (the
  captures before the 10th home-born doe) and LARD's leak during founding. If established herds stay large,
  3201-4000 will be insensitive to anything from about 5 to 30.
- **That is measured only in a probe.** `_homeDoes` is not in the year rows. camp_probe.mjs is a probe, and recorded
  numbers come from committed tools reading the record. Before launch, add an output-only year-row counter (camp-days
  self-maintaining, and home-born breeding does), identity-checked as for 8dd84a2.
- **Pre-state the sensitivity reading.** If established herds are self-maintaining on 95% or more of camp-days in
  3201-4000, selfDoes is not a lever for the window and needs no sweep. If not, sweep 5 and 20.
- **Say what 10 stands for.** The quoted justification ("once a herd and captives can be distinguished") is about
  recognition, but a herder knows a captive from the day he brings it home. What the threshold encodes is demographic
  sufficiency: the herd replaces itself without captures. Ten breeding does at about one kid each a year gives about
  five doelings a year. Whether that replaces the herd's doe losses at REF's mortality is checkable from the record,
  and that would anchor 10 better than "a first choice".
- **Small, and arguably right:** the rule toggles nightly, with no hysteresis, so a herd near 10 switches back and
  forth. An opportunist would do much the same. Note it.
- **Small:** with buckManagement 1 the sires are the oldest adult bucks (params.js:307, world.js:1176-1177), larder captives included.
  A captured adult buck can be a breeding sire from his first rut, with his sire credit set to 0, until the cull eats
  him. LARD's leak (the probe's ~0.1 first breedings per capture) is that race. So its strength depends on when the
  cull runs relative to the rut, not on the rule's intent. Report capBred / capIn by sex, which the scorer already
  plans.

## 6. Smaller points
- **Capture itself selects on R (minor).** The approach test bolts at 0.6 × R × fear per step (world.js:755-760), so
  founders are a low-R sample: CTLDEEP's founders' R was 0.791 at 301-400 against a wild near 0.86. Prediction 2's "no
  rule reads R" should say "no herder rule". Report founders' R against the wild's at founding, so the capture
  filter's share of any R gap is visible.
- **The decision rule lacks branches (minor):**
  - FLAT closes (prediction 1 fails): the rules are then read against an already-closed baseline;
  - an arm fails to establish (NUIS30 is the candidate, point 2);
  - seeds split.
  "R near the wild" in branch 1 is undefined. Use prediction 2's −0.30, as a difference from FLAT.
- **Two chances at branch 1 (minor).** "LARD or TAPER" gives two arms to pass. State which result goes in the paper if
  only one passes, and why.
- **Context arms (note).** REF and LOADLO differ from FLAT in load, scaling and loss together. exp010's LOAD1 is the
  only same-load comparison (point 3). Name it as context beside the other two.

## On the amendment (945313b, with counters 1dd4fa7), before any run
It answers points 1-6. I read the amendment and the counter diff (output only: `looseDeaths` at a loose herd goat's
death, `selfMaintDays` per camp-night when selfDoes > 0). Two small things remain:
- **Arm mean or per-seed count.** "Closed" and "diverged" are judged on arm means, with per-seed counts beside them.
  State which governs when they disagree, for example an arm mean at depth 10.5 with 4 of 7 established seeds closed.
  I'd let the count govern and call the arm "mixed" when neither side has 6 or more of its established seeds.
- **exp009's pooled C as the reference for exp011 (accepted, with one caveat).** The expected pull on an allele is the
  mutation process alone, whatever the Ne, so C's pooled fraction carries over. Two things don't carry over: meioses
  per window, which depend on generation time, and how many alleles sit near a bound. If exp011's arms differ from
  exp009's in mean breeding age, scale the expectation by the ratio of generations over 3201-4000. Report the arms'
  generation times beside the restated breadth.

## On the viability counters (434ee46), before exp011 runs
I read the diff: no random draws, and one new flag (`firstSired`) that nothing else reads. Output-only is plausible.
Three points:
- **Record the identity check (minor).** The commit message and EXPLOG don't state one, and exp011, plus exp010's later
  runs, now run on this code rather than on the registered 1dd4fa7. Put its result (years, seed, rows differing) in
  EXPLOG beside the provenance.
- **exp010 now spans two code versions (minor).** Its earlier runs lack the counters and its later runs have them. The
  counters exist only for the arms and seeds that happened to start later, a non-random subset, so they should not
  be read for exp010 at all. Each run's code commit should be in the raw entry's provenance, as for exp009.
- **Read birth against first breeding by cohort, not by year (moderate, for the analysis).** The effect sought is
  small. In CTLDEEP the opposition on B balances a pull of about r × distance ≈ 0.0018 × 0.3 ≈ 0.0005 a generation.
  First breeders in year t were born about 2.8 years earlier (median first kidding in the herd). A same-year
  comparison therefore carries the population's secular change over that lag, about the pull itself per generation,
  which is the same size as the effect. Compare first breeders in year t with births in year t minus the lag, or take
  the difference net of the window's trend. Anything this small also needs the pooled interval over seeds, not a per-run
  reading.

## On amendment 8 (d68a794, Chris's decision), before any run
Accepted. If relaxed selection is the predicted change, a null that subtracts the change does not test the theory;
it removes the theory's effect. My point 1 above, that "diverged" can be met by relaxation alone, is now by design.
The fixes to the nulls from amendments 1 and 3-7 are exploratory with them. One consequence for the paper's wording
remains (moderate):
- **Breadth at cost 0 has no direction of its own.** With no genome cost, a relaxed trait moves toward 0.5. That
  direction is set by the mutation model, uniform steps clamped at 0 and 1 (`genome.js:73`), and not by any biology
  in the model. Where exp011's gaps run toward 0.5, the paper should call them relaxed selection, with the direction
  coming from the mutation model. It should not call them decay or selection by herders. selSire/selDam on R and the
  within-arm split, which stand, are what can show herders selecting. exp012 asks whether a cost gives relaxation a
  direction; until it reports, that wording is the one the results support.
