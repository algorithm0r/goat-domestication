## 1. Four of the six breadth traits in LARD and TAPER are the wild still settling after the burn-in (major; fatal to citing breadth 6 as herd change until checked)

**The wild moves the same way in every arm.** The figures are wild change against the 301-400 founders, over

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| S | −0.247 | −0.264 | −0.260 | −0.250 | −0.256 | −0.247 |

This holds whatever the herders do, and whether the herd is open or closed. On all four traits the wild moves away
from 0.5:
- Founders' A, H and W sit above 0.5, and the wild rises.
- Founders' S sits below 0.5, and the wild falls.

So mutation is not what drives the wild here; selection in the wild is.

**It is the wild's own course under wolves.** In the epoch pilot, WT has no humans and no herding. Its wild moved

- W: 0.566 to 0.732.

From 2901-3000 to 3901-4000, every core trait stayed within 0.05. exp011's founders are drawn at 301-400, in the

By exp007's more-than-half rule (EXPLOG, exp007 scored, exploratory), the wild carries each of these gaps. In FLAT and
NUIS, the open herds follow the wild, and the gaps stay under 0.07.

**What the herd itself changed:**
- LARD: T +0.353 and B −0.143.
- TAPER: T +0.390 and R −0.177.

So the gaps carried by herd change number 2 per arm, below the decision rule's 3.

exp007 already found this in CTLDEEP: "the deep herd stayed near the founders while the wild moved" on S, W and A.
REF's A and S gaps are wild-carried too (herd −0.060 and −0.073).

**Why the burn-in check missed it.** The burn-in rule tested S0late against S0 on the R gap, the B gap and herd size
only. S0 is an open herd (depth 8-10, about 7 captures a year) that tracks the wild on A, S, H and W. The check could
not have failed on this.

**What stands.** The registered scoring is correct as registered: breadth counts gaps, and amendment 8 counts them
whatever their cause. But the paper can't say that closure by herder culture gave the herd broad change. The
following are open:
- **One reading:** the gaps are the herd being sheltered from the wolves' selection on the wild.
- **The other:** they are the herd's founders preserving a wild that had not finished settling. With a wild settled
  at founding, there would be nothing for the herd not to follow.

The record doesn't separate the two.

**Fix:**
- **Exp011's entry:** add which pool carries each breadth trait, as exp007's entry did, so the counts above come from
  a tool.
- **The paper:** until the check below runs, report closure's effect as herd change (T, plus B or R). Don't report it
  as breadth 6.
- **The check, the steward's call:** run LARD and TAPER (REF if the queue allows) with herding after the wild has
  settled, as S0late did: humans at 1900 and herding at 2000. Compare them with the year-400 arms at equal time since
  herding, on the A, S, H and W gaps. This check can fail either way:
  - If those gaps vanish, the closed-herd breadth in exp007, exp009 and exp011 is mostly burn-in.
  - If they persist, the shelter reading stands.
- **A pending check of my own:** split_tab at 801-1000, 1601-1800 and 2401-2600 for FLAT, LARD and TAPER. It is
  queued in cpuslot behind exp012. It would show whether the wild's change after 400 comes mostly before about 1500,
  as WT's does. It is a supporting check; the finding doesn't rest on it.

## 2. TAPER's R diverged by the breadth threshold; "R near the wild" is the −0.30 threshold, not "unchanged" (moderate, for wording)
- **The gap:** TAPER's R gap is −0.117 (0.031), and R counts in its breadth. Its herd moved R −0.177 against the
  wild's −0.060.
- **Against FLAT:** paired, it is −0.087 (0.034), which excludes 0.
- **LARD:** the herd moved R −0.120.
- **Selection among breeders:** selSire and selDam R are 0.000 and −0.000 in TAPER, and they can be read there (point
  3). So the R shift isn't fecundity selection on R. From founders' R about 0.78, it fits relaxation toward 0.5.

**"Without any rule reading reactivity"** (branch 1's wording) also overstates what was switched off. In every
exp011 arm, goats still bolt, and the herder sees it and fetches them back. Tether, capture's approach test and
retrieval still read R (amendment 1 lists them). What FLAT, LARD and TAPER lack is loss.

**Fix:** "R near the wild" should read "R gap above −0.30, a fifth of REF's in TAPER". The paper should say the herds
diverged with no loss of bolting goats, not with no rule reading reactivity.

## 3. The selection-on-R counter cannot do the job amendment 1 gave it in open herds (moderate)
The parent pool is the camp's adult herd goats at the start of the year (`world.js:1488-1496`). Every herd kid's
paternal half is scored against it unless its sire was wild (`world.js:1502`). Two consequences:
- **Sires that joined during the year are measured against a pool that lacks them.** A buck captured during the year
  who sires that year is one such case. In open herds, the sire differential therefore carries inflow as well as
  selection. LOAD1 (captive-sire share 0.30) gives +0.014, and REF (0.064) gives +0.005. Both are positive while R
  falls.
- **Loss before breeding age is invisible.** Goats that bolt and are lost as kids or yearlings never enter the pool.

Amendment 1 registered the counter "so selection on R is told apart from captives being eaten first through their
fear". In open herds it can't do that. In LARD and TAPER, with captive-sire share 0.005 and 0.003, it reads cleanly.

**Fix:** read selection on R only for the closed arms. Label the open arms' values (FLAT, NUIS, REF, LOAD1)
"confounded with inflow; blind to loss before breeding age". The paper must not cite REF's +0.005 as "no selection on
R".

**Question:** can a buck captured this year sire before the next year's pool? If captives must wait out a year, the
first consequence shrinks to sires moved between camps.

established and tamer than its wild".

## On point 3, after the analysis session's code reading (5dce2dd's exchange)
The pool is taken at day 0 of the birth year (world.js:129), after most conceptions (rut centred on day 330, gestation
150), so most captive sires are in it; the question in point 3 is answered no for the main case. The pool lacks the
sire mainly when he leaves the herd between conception and day 0 (culled, dead, escaped, moved), or sires in the
