# Review of the exp022 prereg, the herdPerHerder sweep (2026-11-27)

## 1. P2 holds almost by construction, and A is ruled out by the record (major)
**The rule.** Above the floor, load = herd / herdPerHerder, with one herder per camp at economy 0 (world.js:228-229,
252). So loadMean is loadHerd / herdPerHerder, apart from camp-days at the 0.25 floor.

**What P2 then tests.** P2 is a statement about herd size:
- the ordering fails at an adjacent pair only if the herd more than doubles when herdPerHerder doubles;
- "HPH160 below half of HPH20" fails only if HPH160's herd is at least four times HPH20's;
- A, every loadMean within ±25% of HPH20's, needs herd size to follow herdPerHerder within ±25% from 5 to 160.

**What the record shows.** Herd size barely moved across loads: LOADLO 261.6, LOAD05 261.9, LOAD1 245.8, REF 287.8
(EXPLOG 6111-6117). Something other than the load sets it. So P2 is close to certain, and its holding is no evidence
for L. A is a contrast with a position the record already rules out. On the steward's question: the ±25% band isn't
the problem; A is.

**Fix:**
- Report the herd's response directly: loadHerd per arm and seed, as a description, not a prediction.
- Keep P2's two parts as a check on the manipulation ("the load differs"), not as a prediction under L.
- Either drop A, or keep it as the reading of that description. Replace it as the alternative with point 2.

## 2. The live alternative is level against rise, and this batch alone can't separate them (major)
**What each arm changes.** herdPerHerder scales the whole load curve. Each arm moves three things together:
- the standing level, loadMean;
- the slope with herd size, 1 / herdPerHerder;
- the herd size at which the floor stops binding, 0.25 × herdPerHerder goats.

So no contrast inside exp022 can say whether the gap follows the level or the rise.
- Decision branch 1, "the gap follows the load", can't tell them apart.
- Branch 2, "the load rule's effect is then not its level, so look at its rise", can't be reached on this batch's
  evidence. A P1 failure with P2 holding is also a failure of the rise, which differs across arms too.

**The record has the other half.** In the fixed-load arms the load doesn't rise, and loadMean is minLoad by
construction (herdPerHerder 1e9):
- LOADLO 0.25, R gap −0.055;
- LOAD05 0.5, −0.104;
- LOAD1 1, −0.247.

No fixed load of 2 or more gives a herd (EXPLOG 6111-6117). LOAD05 at the current code is reproduced by exp021's
LOAD05c (be9e46a).

**So the test the prereg wants is a comparison at matched loadMean.**
- If the standing level sets the gap, an HPH arm whose loadMean falls in 0.25-1 sits near the fixed-load gap at that
  load.
- If the rise matters, it sits below that gap.
- Above loadMean 1 no fixed-load herd exists to compare with. That is the record's own evidence that the rise matters
  at least for establishment (exp010's decision rule, EXPLOG 5672-5674, and its scored reading, 6135).

**Fix:**
- Register that comparison. Say which fixed-load arms it uses, and how an HPH arm is matched to one: for example, its
  pooled loadMean within a stated distance of 0.25, 0.5 or 1, read per seed against that arm's seeds. Say what counts
  as "near" and "below".
- Rewrite decision branch 2 on it.
- It needs no new runs.

## 3. The decision rule leaves cases out (moderate)
It has three branches: both hold; P2 holds and P1 fails; and P2 fails as A.
- **Not covered:** P2 fails, but not as A, whether P1 holds or fails.
- **"Within ±25%"** doesn't say whether every established arm must lie in the band.

**Fix:** a branch or a default for every combination, written after points 1 and 2 are settled.

## 4. The low end is both descriptive and inside the orderings (moderate)
**The text.**
- HPH5 and HPH10 are "descriptive, not scored".
- But P1's and P2's orderings run "from HPH160 down to the smallest established herdPerHerder", which takes in HPH5
  and HPH10 if they establish.

**Neither defines an established arm.** exp010 read levels "with at least 2 established seeds" (EXPLOG 5691-5692).

**Fix:**
- Say whether the orderings run over HPH20-HPH160 only, or over every established arm.
- Define an established arm by a seed count.

**The wording.** "A herd of 10-20 goats per camp already works at load 1-2": at HPH5 a herd of 20 is at load 4
(world.js:229). Say "a herd of 10 per camp works at load 2 at HPH5 and at 1 at HPH10".

## 5. Strict ordering, and the P1 threshold (minor)
**Ordering.** Ordering at every adjacent pair has no tolerance, so a reversal of any size over five pairs fails it.
exp010 held the continuous measures strict too (EXPLOG 5691-5694), so this is consistent. Say so in the text: "a
reversal of any size fails it".

**The 0.20 threshold** (the steward's first question). It's acceptable as a floor: it is the move exp021 asked for, and
it is about a third of the steward's 0.581. But if HPH160's loadMean falls near 0.5, the fixed-load record (LOAD05
−0.104 against REF −0.636) makes this part close to certain under either reading of point 2. Its information is
whether HPH160 behaves like a fixed load. Keep 0.20; the ordering and point 2 carry the test.

## 6. The comparison and the identity check (minor)
