# Review of the exp018 pre-registration (2026-11-24, before any result)

On EXPLOG "## exp018" (281eae4), its code (src/world.js sampleDensity, src/datamanager.js `density`) and
tools/exp018.jsonl. No exp018 result exists. I read the code at 281eae4 and did not run anything.

**What I checked and found right.**
- **The job list.** It has 32 jobs at 4000 years. Against its source job lists:
  - SPR05 and SPR10 equal exp017's job of the same seed in every argument.
  - REF s5-8 equal exp006's REF jobs. REF s1-4 add `exchangeN=0` to exp005's, which is the default (params.js:338).
  - HUNT equals REF of the same seed except `herdingAt=1e9`, which is also the default (params.js:222).
- **The counter's arithmetic.**
  - It runs right after `buildIndex(false)` at sub-tick daySteps >> 1 (world.js:473-474). No goat moves or dies between
    the rebuild and the sample, and it draws nothing.
  - For a herd goat, own is `cnt − wild` in its cell, and own9 is `a9 − w9` over its 3×3. For a wild goat they are
    `wildCount`. Both include the goat itself.
  - The cell counts (`d[c][3]`) are summed over days like the goat samples, so perCell is goat-samples over occupied
    cell-samples.
  - `herd < 0` is the class test used by buildIndex itself (world.js:1450), so a feral (herd set to −1 on escape,
    world.js:833, 930) counts as wild.
- **HUNT has no herd.** `managing()` is false (world.js:163), so there is no herder and no capture.

## 1. The herd class mixes goats kept at camp with goats out with the herder (major; your state definitions)
step() sends a sheltered or kept-at-camp herd goat toward its camp cell every sub-tick (animals.js:273-274). In every arm
of this batch that covers two groups (kidShelter 1, tetherNew 1; rutGuard is 0, so guarding adds none):
- herd kids under shelterAge 30 days (animals.js:250);
- every herd goat with R × fearOfHumans ≥ captureFear 0.35, tethered "until it habituates" (animals.js:252-253).

At midday these goats sit on the camp cell, up to herdRange 8 cells from the herder and the goats grazing with him. The
counter puts them in the same class, and a pile on one cell weighs heavily in a goat-weighted mean: each of k goats on
the cell scores k. Two readings depend on that pile's size, and it differs by arm and state.
- **Prediction 2.** SPR05's small state is its fearful, high-R phase, which is when most of a small herd should be
  tethered. Its herd own could then exceed the established state's because a tethered pile sits on one cell, not
  because the herd is crowded at pasture. The prediction could hold or fail on the wrong mechanism.
- **Chris's question.** A spook rule acts on goats that can be spooked. The kept goats are exactly the ones the herder
  cannot spook (walksWithHerder, animals.js:386-389).

The entry's "a herd sits in at most 25 cells" is also not so. Kept goats are at the camp, kids with mothers follow the
mother, and loose run-offs move as wild goats. The 25 cells are the keepRadius 2 box around the herder only.

**Fix (before queueing; output only):**
- Split the herd class in sampleDensity by `walksWithHerder` into three:
  - **with herder** (walksWithHerder true);
  - **kept** (isSheltered or isKeptAtCamp);
  - **other herd** (kids following a mother, loose run-offs, captives being led).
- Do the same in the cell count `d[c][3]`, or drop perCell for the herd sub-classes.
- Read predictions 1-2 on the with-herder class, with the kept share printed beside them.
- This needs a fresh 700-year identity probe, since the split adds a function call but no draw. It is the same probe
  as now.

## 2. Prediction 4's contrast is herding as a package, not the herd's gene flow; the decision sentence overreaches (major)
HUNT and REF differ in more than whether a herd exists. With economy 0, at each camp with a herd the first person
becomes the herder, and only the rest draw against huntEffort to hunt (world.js:244-255). In HUNT all five draw. So
after a camp has a herd, REF's wild is hunted by fewer people a day than HUNT's. Captures also remove wild kids in REF
and not in HUNT.

"If it holds, the wild's lower R in REF is the herd's doing, not hunting's" therefore does not follow. If HUNT's wild
R is higher, a share of that can be less hunting in REF: hunting raises the wild's R (EXPLOG line 643; exp017's
pre-herding bins 0.678 to 0.819). That is hunting's doing, through the herder taken off the hunt.

**Fix:**
- Print huntTries per arm over 301-400 and 3201-4000 (the counter exists, world.js:721), and wild goat-samples per day
  (the counter's wild n) beside the wild R.
- Word the reading as: "if it holds, the wild's R in REF sits below its level under hunting alone. The difference is
  herding's doing, which includes its gene flow, captures and one person per camp moved from hunting to herding. This
  batch does not separate them."
- Drop "not hunting's".

**Two more on prediction 4.**
- **The seed pairing is arbitrary after year 400.** HUNT_seedS and REF_seedS share every draw until herding begins,
  and then diverge. "6 or more of 8 seeds" is a sign count over pairs that mean nothing after 400. Say whether 5 of 8
  is "fails" or "unread". Better, give a criterion on the two sets of 8 per-seed values, and say what difference is
  too small to read. exp017's LOAD05 plateau moved by up to about 0.04 within a seed (my 497d6ea point 2).
- **The tool does not read HUNT yet.** wildr_tab takes J and the established list from scored.txt and splits on
  histHerd. HUNT has no J, no scored.txt and no herd. The measure should say how HUNT's wild R is read: the all-goat
  R, with a check that histHerd is empty in every HUNT row (a check that stops on a REF run). It should also say
  which REF is read: exp018's reruns or exp017's, which are equal if the identity holds.

## 3. "Established" is ambiguous, and "small" is not load_tab's waited state (moderate; your state definitions)
- **Established.** "3201-4000 in seeds with herd >= 100 over that window" could mean the window mean, every row, or the
  trailing 100-year mean at every point. Name one. Using load_tab's J (J ≤ 3201) and its follow-up rule would tie it
  to the seeds exp017 scored as established.
- **Small differs from load_tab's waited state in two ways,** although the entry says they are the same.
  - **After J.** load_tab counts only points before J (load_tab.py:186-191). The entry's "years after 500" also takes
    any post-J crash below 60, which is a different state.
  - **The trailing lag.** The 100-year mean is trailing (jump_tab.herd_series, lines 23-29). In the years right after
    a jump, the mean is still under 60 while the herd itself is already near 100. SPR05's jumps are quick, so the small
    state takes in large-herd years.

  **Fix:** define small by the year's own herd count (under 60), before J, after 500. Or keep the trailing mean and
  drop the 100 years before J. Say which.
- **A floor on rows.** REF waited 0 of 8 in exp017, so REF's small state may be a handful of rows per seed, and some
  of those small-state years have no herd goat at all. State a minimum, say 10 recorded rows and a herd goat-sample
  in each, below which a seed-state is "not read". Prediction 1's "every arm-state with a herd, every seed" then
  ranges over the seed-states that meet it.

## 4. Prediction 3's 25% band has no anchor (moderate; your 25%)
Nothing in the entry says why 25%, or what wild own's seed-to-seed spread is. Wild own is a goat-weighted mean set
mostly by group size (gregariousness over socialRange 4), so its spread in HUNT is unknown before the run. The
prediction is also an equivalence claim ("does not change"), which needs a margin that means something for its use.
- **Anchor the band.** Either:
  - state the band now as the one that matters for the rule Chris weighs (a spook chance that rises with goats
    nearby: what change in the count would change the rule's effect noticeably?); or
  - define "within" as inside HUNT's per-seed range over 3201-4000, which the run supplies.

  Say whether the comparison is arm mean against arm mean or per seed, and how the ratio is taken (|x / HUNT − 1| ≤
  0.25).
- **Read all and all9 beside own.** The rule Chris describes counts other goats nearby, of either kind. A wild goat's
  `all` counts herd goats in its cell, so own cannot show a wild goat living beside a herd, and all9 can.
- **Report the wild population beside it.** Captures remove wild kids in REF, and ferals add to the wild. Wild own
  moves with both.
- **The mean is over the whole map.** It dilutes what happens near camps. The wildNearCamp counter (world.js:913-914)
  already gives wild goats within herdRange of a camp. Read it as the local term, descriptively.

## 5. The standing-control rule needs a tighter scope (moderate; your wording)
- **"Code that can change goats, wolves or hunting" covers nearly all code,** and who decides is not said. Write
  "every batch, unless the HUNT check below passes".
- **The 700-year probe misses changes that act later.** It also misses changes that bite only when the wild is in a
  state it first reaches after 700. One 4000-year HUNT seed 1 run, compared on every record, costs an eighth of the
  arm and covers the whole record. Use that, or say why 700 years is enough.
- **Arguments count as well as code.** If a batch changes any base argument HUNT carries, HUNT reruns at the new base.
  Say so.
- **Name what a batch reads against HUNT.** Do this in each registration that includes it, or say "available, not
  read". A control nobody reads is decoration.
- **When HUNT is cited instead of rerun,** name its experiment, run and code commit, and give the check's result in
  the raw entry.

## 6. A free identity check: all four arms of a seed are one run until year 400 (minor)
The arms of a seed differ only in herdingAt, herdPerHerder, minLoad and alarmSpread. None of these acts before
herding, and the IBD relabel falls at min(herdingAt, ibdFrom) = 400 in every arm (world.js:136; ibdFrom 400). So
HUNT_seedS, REF_seedS, SPR05_seedS and SPR10_seedS should agree on every shared key through year 399.
- **Compare them** (cmp_years over the rows before 400). It is a check that could fail, at no run cost.
- **The 301-400 baseline is then one per seed, printed four times.** Say so, and don't read it as four agreeing
  arms.
- **Year 400 itself** is the first herding year in REF and SPR, so the baseline should stop at 399 or say how year 400
  is handled. The entry's 301-400 includes it.

## 7. Minor
- **Rerun failures other than REF's.** The entry says what happens if REF does not reproduce, but not if SPR05 or
  SPR10 don't. Their code differs from exp017's only by the counter, so a difference means the counter is not output

  code, and flag those readings.
- **Prediction 1 is close to true by construction** for the with-herder class: keepRadius 2 holds it in 25 cells

- **"Own includes itself."** For a rule on other goats nearby, own − 1 is the count. Print it, or say so in the table

- **Midday only.** Herder spooks happen by day, and wolf alarms come at dawn and night, when goats bed down. The counter
  says nothing about crowding then. Name the scope in the table.

  a rule might read that. Say the rule's radius is not chosen.
