# Review of the exp010 pre-registration (2026-11-20, before results)

On EXPLOG "## exp010" (4aaf978) and tools/exp010.jsonl. The 32 jobs set `herdPerHerder=1e9` and `minLoad` 0.5, 1, 2
and 3, as the entry says. The design answers Chris's question and is a fair start on design-review finding 1(c). No
major problems; five points, the first two worth adding before results.

### 1. State the filter strength as an observable rate, or the decision rule's question can't be answered (moderate)
The decision rule ends on "how strong a filter herders would plausibly impose". The load is an internal multiplier:
spook chance per goat per day = herderAlarm 0.05 × R × fearOfHumans × load (`animals.js:363`). No herder or
ethnographer can say whether 2 is plausible. To be compared with anything outside the model, each level needs the rates
it produces in herd goats per year: run-offs, run-offs fetched back, and goats lost (flow_tab's `esc`). Register those
per level, beside the herd and the gaps, so the paper can say "divergence needs losses of x% of the herd a year" and
look for herd-loss figures. That makes the plausibility question answerable.

### 2. Add a readout for design-review Q1: does a fixed load keep the long small state? (moderate)
"Time to establishment" is listed. In exp007, herds with the load fixed low or with no spooking at all were
established at once (left-censored at 501), while REF-type arms such as RAND and noProv sat at 20-50 goats for
thousands of years. At fixed loads of 1-3 there are two possibilities. Herds may wait and then jump: then the small
state does not need the load to scale with herd size. Or they may never establish, or establish at once: then the
waiting is the scaling rule's doing. Add a stated reading for each, so exp010 also answers Q1.

### 3. Load and herderAlarm enter only as a product (note)
At a fixed load the spook chance depends on herderAlarm × load alone (herderAlarm is read only at `animals.js:363`).
So this sweep is also a herderAlarm sweep at the default load, and a separate fixed-load herderAlarm sweep would repeat
it. Say so in the entry; it saves runs in the re-plan. Under the size-scaled rule the two are not interchangeable:
herderAlarm scales the whole curve, and herdPerHerder moves where it starts rising.

### 4. The transition may fall between 3 and 4.8, with no level there (minor)
LOADHI (4.8) let no herd survive, and the prediction expects at most 4 of 8 to establish at 3. If 3 still persists, the
edge lies somewhere in 3-4.8 and the batch won't locate it. State in advance that a follow-up at 4 would be run if 3
persists in 6 or more of 8. It is cheap, and it keeps that later level from being chosen after the data.

### 5. Cross-experiment comparisons rest on the identity checks (minor, already covered)
exp010 runs at 6367599, exp007's LOADLO and LOADHI at a0a2e12, and REF at exp005/exp006's code. The intervening code
changes are output-only counters (8dd84a2) and the default-off C switch (92d8f21), each identity-checked over 500
years. The exp007 code reproduces exp005 over all 4000 years (EXPLOG 710f1eb). That is enough for pooling. The entry
should cite those checks next to the comparison, so a reader of exp010 alone can see why the arms are comparable.
