## 1. "HPH10 … continues it" reads a descriptive arm into the ordering (moderate)
**The text.** For Chris, lines 137-138: "from 20 to 160, the more goats one herder watches, the smaller the R gap.
HPH10, outside the scored arms, continues it at −0.659."

**What is registered.** 53ee45e point 4: "HPH5 and HPH10 stay descriptive, and the orderings do not take them in."
The prereg's low end asks for establishment, J and herd only.

**What is printed.** The per-seed ranges overlap:
- HPH10's R gap is −0.680 to −0.622 (hph_tab.txt 14-21);
- HPH20's is −0.658 to −0.611 (22-29).

So "continues" claims an ordering step that the registration excludes and that the seeds don't separate.

**Fix:** "HPH10, which the registration leaves out of the orderings, has an R gap of −0.659 (−0.680 to −0.622 per
seed), against HPH20's −0.636 (−0.658 to −0.611)."

## 2. "The goats a herder watches" names the wrong quantity (moderate)
**The text.**
- For Chris, line 126: "The R gap follows the number of goats a herder watches."
- Line 137: "the more goats one herder watches, the smaller the R gap".

**What is printed.** In every arm read, the herder watches about the same herd: one camp's herd is 93.3 to 101.3
(hph_tab.txt 60-63). What the sweep varies is herdPerHerder, the number of goats a herder can watch before spooking
rises (params.js:249). Through it, the sweep varies the load.

Read as written, Chris could take this to mean that bigger herds have smaller gaps. Bullet 2 says the opposite.

**Fix:**
- Line 126: "The R gap follows how many goats a herder can watch before spooking rises (herdPerHerder)."
- Line 137: "the more goats a herder can watch before spooking rises, and so the lower the load, the smaller the R gap."
