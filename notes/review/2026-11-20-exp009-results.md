# Review of exp009's results (2026-11-20)

On EXPLOG "exp009 results (scored)" (9cd6950) and results/exp009_mutation-pull/ (scored.txt, split_founders.txt,
flow_3201-4000.txt). The scoring follows the registered rule and both amendments as written. Validity passes. The
pooled C passes the width bar. "No registered outcome applies" is correct, and the rule's gap is my error: I set the
mutational range at 0.3-0.6 and wrote no reading for a pooled C above it. C relaxed further than I predicted (0.84).

The registered *interpretation* ("pull partly opposed by selection in the herd") is the part that does not hold. The
first point says why. It reverses the mechanism, refutes my hypothesis (exp007-results §3) for most traits, and
changes exp011's divergence rule before its results.

## 1. The herd lags C because of gene flow, not because selection opposes the pull (major; my hypothesis, mostly refuted)
C relaxes in the herd *and* in the wild; nothing holds it anywhere. A, H, T, R and B are held in the wild by selection.
So a herd that receives wild genes is tied to a wild that does not relax, and it lags C even with no selection in the
herd at all. The paired C − trait interval measures that lag. It cannot say what produced it.

**What the pull alone could do against the measured inflow** (my arithmetic, my assumptions):
- **Inflow.** CTLDEEP's depth, 19.61, is a capped mean: WV_DEPTH 24, last slot open-ended (animals.js:24,
  datamanager.js:92). With geometric depths, a capped mean of 19.61 means wild genes arrive at m ≈ 0.015 per
  generation. Captives are counted as wild although the wild there is 0.28 herd-descended (flow: feral 0.284), so
  the inflow of wild-type genes is nearer 0.011. These figures are realized ancestry; conception counts would give
  cdam/2 ≈ 0.043.
- **Pull rate.** C closed 0.843 of its distance to 0.5 over about 3250 years. At 3-5 years a generation, that is a
  relative rate r ≈ 0.0017-0.0029 per generation.
- **Balance.** At balance, a herd under pull and inflow alone sits r/(m + r) of the way from the wild to 0.5. The pull
  alone gives the observed CTLDEEP gaps only if the inflow is at most:

| | A | H | T | R | B |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gap (herd − wild change) | −0.204 | −0.206 | +0.149 | −0.133 | −0.097 |
| pull alone needs m ≤ | 0.001-0.0017 | 0.001-0.0017 | 0.0021-0.0035 | 0.0013-0.0022 | 0.0052-0.0087 |
| pull's share of the gap at m 0.011-0.015 | 0.16-0.33 | 0.16-0.33 | 0.23-0.46 | 0.18-0.36 | 0.41-0.84 |

**Reading.** For A, H, R and T, the inflow would have to be 3-15 times below what depth shows. So in CTLDEEP most of
each gap is held by selection in the herd, and that selection points *toward* 0.5, the same way as the pull, against
inflow from a wild held away from it. Most of the lag behind C is gene flow, not selection opposing the pull. The pull
contributes about a fifth to a half of these gaps. B is unresolved: the pull may carry most of its gap.

**What this means:**
- My exp007-results §3 hypothesis is refuted as the main cause of the deep herd's own change on A, H, R and T. It
  stands as a minority share, and possibly as most of B.
- EXPLOG's per-trait reading "pull partly opposed by selection in the herd" should carry a note. The paired test was
  registered and is correctly scored, but in a herd with inflow it does not identify the mechanism.
- **Open question (unanchored).** The CTL arms have no spooking and no keep value: herderAlarm 0 and a random cull of
  0.13 (EXPLOG 5390). What selects R, A, H and B toward 0.5, and T upward, strongly enough to hold gaps of 0.1-0.2
  against inflow? It is five traits with different mechanisms, all pointing toward the midpoint, and that pattern
  deserves suspicion. A first look, exploratory and from the year rows: selDam (world.js:1477-1495) on these traits in
  CTLDEEP. Dams are the half of the parents not chosen by ancestry.
- **Caveats.**
  - Depth is capped, and depths are not exactly geometric.
  - Generation time is assumed; please compute it from the record. The exp011 amendment 2 measure, mean parent age at
    birth, would do.
  - A trait with more alleles near a bound than C is pulled faster: A and H have founders' genotype SD 0.108 and 0.109,
    against C's 0.080. That widens the shares above by perhaps 1.5×, which still leaves A, H and R mostly selection.

## 2. REF's gaps are selection-held, B included: "B inside C's interval" must not reach the paper as "mutational" (major for the paper)
REF's depth, 14.69, gives m ≈ 0.044 (≈ 0.038 after the feral share of 0.128). R and T fractions above 1 (2.46, 1.45)
are selection by definition: the pull cannot carry a trait past 0.5. For the others, the pull alone needs:
- B: gap −0.295, needs m ≤ 0.0005-0.0008, which is 50-75 times below the measure;
- A: −0.148, needs m ≤ 0.002-0.0033;
- H: −0.083, needs m ≤ 0.0045-0.0076.

The REF B fraction (0.78) is close to C's (0.81), but that is a coincidence of two different processes. A free marker
relaxes, and REF's herd is held by its own selection against 4% inflow a generation. So the body-size result
(herd-average growth credit) survives exp009. The author should not cite exp009 as showing B is mutational.

## 3. exp011's restated divergence rule uses the wrong null for herds with inflow (major; before exp011 results; partly my error)
The amendment (945313b), as I asked in d265982 point 1, counts a trait only if its herd change exceeds C's fraction ×
(0.5 − F). That is the right null for a herd with no inflow. For a herd with inflow, point 1 shows the expectation is
about r/(m + r) of that, a fifth or less. Applied to REF, the rule drops three gaps that point 2 shows are selection:
- B: herd change −0.291 against an expectation of −0.314;
- A: −0.060 against −0.193;
- H: −0.023 against −0.202.

A rule that scores REF's body-size gap as not diverged is miscalibrated.

**Fix, before exp011 results:**
- For arms with inflow (depth flat over 3201-4000), the null for a trait's herd–wild gap is the arm's own C
  herd–wild gap, paired within run, plus r/(m + r) × (0.5 − wild mean), with m from depth.
- Keep the C-fraction expectation only for herds whose depth rises through the window, i.e. closed herds (TAPER after
  closure).
- Calibrate first: score REF, CTLDEEP and CTLRAND with the restated rule, before any exp011 outcome is read. REF must
  come out diverged on B.

## 4. Depth is capped at 23, so "closed" and the depth reading saturate (moderate; exp011)
wildDepth and herdAncDepth sum over WV_DEPTH 24 slots, the last open-ended (animals.js:24, 511). CTLDEEP's 19.61 is
already near the ceiling, a fully closed herd (TAPER) will sit at about 23, and any threshold above about 20 cannot be
reached. exp011's arms use sireRule oldest, so raising WV_DEPTH would change their output, not their dynamics. But the
jobs are queued, so read depth through the capped-geometric conversion to m given above, and report m beside depth. A
committed tool should do the conversion. The open-ended share in herdWildAnc is a cap-free closure signal.

## 5. CTLRAND meets exp011's "closed" and tracks the wild (moderate; exp011 calibration)
CTLRAND has depth 10.15 and captive-sire share 0.060. That passes exp011's "closed" (depth ≥ 10 and share ≤ 0.10),
yet its largest herd–wild gap is 0.026. CTLDEEP shares CTLRAND's herd environment with about a sixth of its inflow
by depth (m 0.015 against 0.086). Its gaps are 5-23 times larger: A −0.204 against −0.012, H −0.206 against −0.009,
R −0.133 against −0.026. A gap scaling as 1/m would give about 6 times, so A and H go well beyond it (a sign of
swamping), and R and B do not. So depth 10 is a known non-divergent point, and branch 2 ("closed but not diverged") is
already realized by a sire rule. Add exp009's 32 CTL runs, and exp007's three CTL arms, to
exp011's cross-experiment breadth-against-m reading. If the steward wants a higher "closed" line, set it now, before
exp011 results, and say it was set after exp009.

- **Genotype SD.** My exp009-prereg point 3 expected C's spread to be wider than A's and H's. At genotype level it is

- **Validity.** The check passes, but its interval (−0.349, +0.200) means it could not have detected a difference

## On exp011 amendment 3 (82d3592), before any run
- **The steward is right about calibration, and I withdraw "REF must come out diverged on B".** That would choose the
  null by the answer expected of it. Validating on the neutral marker is the right principle.
- **As written, though, the C validation cannot fail (moderate).** The null contains "the arm's own C herd-wild gap",
  so predicting C's gap with it predicts C from itself. The other term, r/(m + r) × (0.5 − wild mean), is the one that
  carries the inflow argument, and C cannot exercise it. C's wild relaxes too, so C's expected gap is about 0 whatever
  m is, and the term is about 0.008 at most for C here (wild C 0.567). No neutral marker in these runs is held away
  from 0.5 in the wild, so the r/(m + r) term has no neutral test in the record. **Fix:**
  - validate the C-gap part on C without C's own gap inside the null (predict about 0, with its interval);
  - say plainly that the r/(m + r) term is model-derived and untested on a neutral marker;
  - report breadth at both ends of its range: the term at 0 (no inflow correction), and the term at the m used.
  A test that could fail would need a neutral marker held in the wild, which would be new code; that is for a re-plan,
  not now.
- **Which m (moderate).** The amendment takes m from the conception records (half the captive-sire share plus half the
  captive-dam share). For CTLDEEP that gives about 0.043. The realized ancestry, from depth with the cap undone, is
  about 0.015. The two differ when captives' lineages do not persist; in CTLDEEP a captive dam's sons never sire. A
  larger m makes the null smaller, so more gaps count as diverged: the conception figure is the anti-conservative
  choice. Report both, and read divergence on the realized one, with a committed conversion from capped depth (or from
  herdWildAnc's slots) to m.

## On exp011 amendment 6 (97d156a), before any run
Making the closed-herd null primary is the right call, with one condition on "it cannot invent divergence" (moderate):
- **The bound is conservative only if its pull fraction is at least what a closed herd shows.** Scaled by the
  first-kid generation ratio, exp009's pooled 0.843 gives about 0.83-0.88 for a herd like CTLDEEP. But CTLDEEP's core
  C actually relaxed to about 0.95 (above). exp011's C is unshifted, so its runs cannot measure their own pull. A
  trait moved by the pull to 0.9 of the way would then pass a 0.85 null. **Fix:** set the primary null's fraction to
  the largest closed-herd relaxation exp009 measured, CTLDEEP's core C, about 0.95. Then the claim "cannot invent
  divergence" holds. Moves away from 0.5, or past it, count under any null.
- **For arms whose ancestry passes the geometric shape test** (REF-like), the secondary inflow null rests on measured
  ancestry and the measured r. That is better identified than "model-dependent" suggests. Under the primary alone,
  REF's B (−0.291 against a closed null of about −0.31 to −0.35) does not count, although point 2 shows it is
  selection-held. The paper should give both readings for geometric arms, and say why they differ.

## On cohort_tab.py (d6ab7a9), before any reading
The cohort shift and the refusal on exp010 are as asked. On the two limits:
- **Remove the residual lag misalignment by trend, and test that on C (moderate).** The births' secular slope over the
  window is in the rows: regress each trait's birth mean on year. Subtract slope × (true lag − rounded lag) from the
  differential. C is the control that can fail, since nothing selects it but it trends in CTLDEEP's herd: after the
  correction, C's birth-to-first-breeding differential must be 0 within its pooled interval. If it is not, the
  correction is wrong and no trait's differential is read. The `--lag 0` reading against the lagged one gives a second
  estimate of slope × lag to compare.
- **Read does first, bucks as secondary (minor).** Bucks borrow the does' lag. Until a first-siring age is recorded,
  label buck differentials as lag-uncertain.
- **Scale (note).** Viability before first breeding is only part of the selection between generations, so a null
  differential on B or T would not show that nothing opposes the pull. It would move the search to fertility after
  first breeding (selDam is tiny and points with the pull) or to culling.

## On cohort_tab (f847f58)
- **The sign is right.** In breed − birth terms the correction is + b·(L − K), and my "subtract" was in birth − breed
  terms. The planted cases are the right ones, including a C with selection that fails the check.
- **Use the mean lag, not the median (minor).** Under a linear trend, a cohort's birth mean is the trend at the mean
  of its ages at first kidding, not at their median. Ages at first kidding are right-skewed, so the median sits below
  the mean, and the correction comes out too small by b × (mean − median). If firstKidStats holds an age sum, use the
  mean. If not, print the median-based correction with a note.
